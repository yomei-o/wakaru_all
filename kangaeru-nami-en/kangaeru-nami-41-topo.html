Thinking in Waves Episode 41 / Part XIII — The Frontier, as Waves

With no road back, there is nothing to bounce into

Topological Waves —
They survive disorder not because they are strong Episode 37 found that in one dimension any disorder, however weak, stops a wave.
★★ Yet some waves do not stop — not from strength, but because there is nowhere to backscatter into.
★ Checked numerically: along one edge branch the group velocity has the same sign at all 42 points. A counter-propagating state does not exist.

Episode 35 showed that light's orbital angular momentum carries a winding number no continuous deformation can change.
★ This episode is about that unchangeable integer counting how many roads a wave has.

The point of §01

Move \(w\) from 0.999 to 1.001 — a change of 0.2 % — and the winding number jumps from 0 to 1.
★ And it never takes a value in between. No 0.5, no 0.9 — zero or one.
★★ The only place it can jump is \(v=w\), that is, where the gap closes. You cannot go from 0 to 1 without closing the gap — that is what "cannot be changed by continuous deformation" means.
★ It is the same kind of quantity as the winding number of an optical vortex in Episode 35 (that one in real space, this one in momentum space).

★ An integer computed from the interior tells you how many states sit at the edge.

The point of §03 — ★★★ the caveat that matters

60 % disorder on the bonds does not move \(E=0\).
0.05 on the sites does.

★ What protects is a symmetry — bond disorder preserves sublattice (chiral) symmetry; site disorder breaks it.
★★ So it is not "topological, hence robust to anything," but robust to a specified thing. Against disorder that breaks that symmetry, it is not robust at all.
★ On Episode 15's scorecard this reads "◎, conditional on a stated assumption."

The numbers are exactly \(E=-\sin k_x\) — a single branch running all the way through the gap from below to above.

★★ And the two edges have opposite signs — at any given energy there is one state on the left edge and one on the right.

The point of §06 — ★★★ the core of this episode

On the left edge all 42 points are negative. A counter-propagating state does not exist.

★ To backscatter you need a state on the same edge, at the same energy, going the other way. If there is none, no amount of disorder gives the wave anywhere to bounce into.
★★ The wave is not strong. There is simply no destination.
★ The counter-propagating state is on the other edge — reaching it means crossing the whole width of the system. The wider the sample, the exponentially smaller that chance.

The point of §07

★ No contradiction. Episode 37's localization comes from interference between forward and backward waves, so with no backward wave it cannot happen.
★★ Episode 37 put it as Pólya's recurrence theorem — a wave that never returns cannot interfere with itself. Here the road back does not exist at all, so the same reason applies even more strongly.
★ But §03's caveat still bites — disorder strong enough to connect the two edges will stop it after all. It is not unconditional.

Exercises

  1. §01 found the winding number never takes 0.5, yet Episode 13 found the order varies continuously. Which is right?
    Show answer
    Both — they are different quantities. Episode 13's order \(s=\frac12\pm\sqrt{\frac14-\lambda}\) is a continuous function of a continuous \(\lambda\). The winding number here is the degree of a map, an integer by definition.
    ★ The difference is local versus once-around: the order describes behaviour at a point, the winding number describes the result of going all the way round. Since you come back to where you started, the total angle can only be an integer multiple of \(2\pi\).
    ★★ They are not unrelated — as §04 showed, the integer jumps where the gap closes, i.e. at a critical point, and Episode 13's order went complex at a critical point too. "Something only happens at criticality" is common to both.
  2. §06 says there is no counter-propagating state. Doesn't that violate conservation of momentum?
    Show answer
    No. Once there is disorder there is no translation symmetry, so momentum is not conserved anyway.
    ★ When a wave bounces, the missing momentum is absorbed by the disorder (the medium). The statement here is not "momentum cannot be transferred" but "there is no state to land in."
    ★★ With no final state the transition rate is zero — not through the matrix element of Fermi's golden rule but through the final density of states. ★ So supply a final state — the other edge — and it can bounce: that is §07's caveat.
  3. §03 found it weak against site disorder. Why is the SSH model called "topological" at all?
    Show answer
    Because the protecting symmetry is declared up front. Topological phases are always classified together with which symmetries are assumed, and SSH belongs to the chiral-symmetric class.
    ★ Within that class the winding number is an integer and cannot be changed continuously — that much is true. Step outside the class and the guarantee is gone.
    ★★ In this series' language: not unconditional robustness but robustness under a stated premise. ★ Just as Episodes 7 and 15 insisted on asking "what did you divide by," one must ask each time which symmetry was assumed.

Summary — it survives not from strength but from having nowhere to go

First we built a quantity that can only be an integer — the SSH winding number. Move \(w\) from 0.999 to 1.001, a change of 0.2 %, and it jumps from 0 to 1, never taking a value in between. It can only jump at \(v=w\), where the gap closes.

And that integer predicts how many states sit at the edges (bulk–boundary correspondence) — two \(E=0\) states, and only when \(w>v\). Their localization length matched \(\xi=1/\ln(w/v)\) with a ratio of 1.00000.

★ But there is a caveat that matters — 60 % disorder on the bonds leaves \(E=0\) untouched, while 0.05 on the sites moves it. What protects is a symmetry (chiral symmetry), not robustness against anything whatsoever.

Two dimensions behaved the same way — the Chern number takes only \(0,\pm1\), and jumps only at \(m=-2,0,+2\): exactly the three points where the gap closes.

★★★ The core — at \(C=1\) a single branch crosses the gap.
Counting the sign of its group velocity: all 42 points negative on the left edge, all positive on the right.
On one edge, a counter-propagating state does not exist.

★ Backscattering needs a state on the same edge, at the same energy, going the other way. With none, disorder has nowhere to send the wave — the wave is not strong; there is simply no destination.

★ This does not contradict Episode 37 — that localization comes from interference between forward and backward waves, and with no backward wave it cannot occur. It restates "a wave that never returns cannot interfere with itself" (Pólya) in a stronger form. ★ Yet disorder strong enough to link the two edges will stop it after all.
