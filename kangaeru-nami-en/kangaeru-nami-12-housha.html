Why only \(1/r\) survives ── just counting $$\text{energy flux}\propto E^2,\qquad \text{sphere area}\propto r^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{total radiated power}\propto E^2r^2$$ $$E\propto1/r^2\ \Rightarrow\ E^2r^2\propto r^{-2}\to0,\qquad E\propto1/r\ \Rightarrow\ E^2r^2=\text{const}\ \textbf{★ survives}$$

Conclusion of §01

A field falling faster than \(1/r\) never reaches infinity.
And the \(1/r\) term appears only in the second derivative (acceleration) ──
★ which is why "radiation requires acceleration". It was a question of order.

Radiated power $$P=\frac{q^2a^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0c^3}$$

03Why the classical atom collapses

The electron in a hydrogen atom moves in a circle. Circular motion is accelerated motion ── so classical electromagnetism says it must radiate and lose energy.

First, naively $$a=\frac{v^2}{a_0}=9.04\times10^{22}\ \text{m/s}^2\ \Rightarrow\ P=4.67\times10^{-8}\ \text{W}$$ $$\frac{|E|}{P}=\frac{2.18\times10^{-18}}{4.67\times10^{-8}}=4.7\times10^{-11}\ \text{s}$$

But as the radius shrinks the acceleration rises and the radiation accelerates. Integrating properly:

Fall time $$t=\frac{a_0^3}{4r_e^2c}=1.556\times10^{-11}\ \text{s}$$

\(r_e=2.818\times10^{-15}\) m (classical electron radius)

Conclusion of §03

In about 16 picoseconds the electron falls into the nucleus.
In classical physics, atoms cannot exist. This was one of the reasons quantum mechanics became necessary.

And in the last 0.1 % of that time the radiated power rises \(10^4\)-fold ── the collapse accelerates.

04An oscillating charge radiates as \(\omega^4\) ── this is what matters

Just square the second derivative $$P\propto\langle\ddot p^2\rangle=\frac{\omega^4p_0^2}{2}$$

★ The second derivative gives \(\omega^2\); squaring gives \(\omega^4\)

This is Rayleigh scattering: the cross-section goes as \(\omega^4\propto1/\lambda^4\).

The heart of §04

The sky is blue because we squared a second derivative.

Air molecules are shaken by light and re-radiate in proportion to their acceleration. That strength goes as \(\omega^4\), so shorter wavelengths scatter more ── blue 4.4 times more than red.

And a red sunset is the same formula turned inside out. With the sun low, light travels far through the atmosphere, the blue is scattered away first, and only red gets through.

Why X-rays appear is the bandwidth theorem again

If an electron stops in \(10^{-18}\) s, the bandwidth is \(\Delta\omega\gtrsim1/(2\Delta t)=5\times10^{17}\) rad/s.
In photon energy, \(\hbar\Delta\omega=329\) keV ── the X-ray band falls out.
★ Bremsstrahlung is an X-ray source as a direct consequence of Episode 9's "short pulses have broad bandwidth".

§06 ── go to third order and causality breaks

The radiation reaction is \(F=\dfrac{q^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0c^3}\dddot x\) ── third order. Therefore:

・it falls under Episode 2's Ostrogradsky instability
・and indeed there are solutions where acceleration begins before the force is applied (pre-acceleration)
・★ which breaks Episode 8's causality

The timescale of pre-acceleration is \(\tau=2r_e/(3c)=6.3\times10^{-24}\) s ── about the light-crossing time of a classical electron radius.
★ "Raise the order too far and causality breaks" actually happens inside classical electromagnetism. This is a failure of the classical theory; quantum electrodynamics resolves it.

Exercises

  1. In this episode's language, why does a charge in uniform motion not radiate?
    Show answer
    Because the acceleration is zero, so the \(1/r\) term never arises. There are only the electrostatic (\(1/r^2\)) and induction (\(1/r^2\)) fields, and for both \(E^2r^2\to0\), so no energy reaches infinity.
    (The usual explanation — that uniform motion can be transformed to rest — says the same thing.)
  2. Explain a red sunset from \(\omega^4\).
    Show answer
    The scattering probability goes as \(\omega^4\), so the longer the path through the atmosphere, the sooner the blue is lost.
    With the sun low, the path is tens of times longer than at the zenith, attenuating blue by the equivalent of \(e^{-4.35\times}\) and letting only red through.
    ★ Blue sky and red sunset are the two sides of one formula ── whether you look at the scattered light or at what is left.
  3. Where does radiation reaction's "pre-acceleration" sit in this series?
    Show answer
    It is a case where Episode 2 (Ostrogradsky: order ≥ 3 is unstable) and Episode 8 (causality) break in the same place at the same time.
    Classical electromagnetism carried a third-order term inside itself ── and, as predicted, produced both an instability and a non-causality.
    ★ It is the most concrete evidence for Episode 2's hypothesis that the order has a ceiling.

Summary　Radiation is a second derivative, squared

Expanding the field of a charge, only the \(1/r\) term is proportional to acceleration and carries energy (it is the only one for which \(E^2r^2\) stays finite). "Radiation requires acceleration" was a question of order.

From the Larmor formula \(P=q^2a^2/(6\pi\varepsilon_0c^3)\), the classical hydrogen atom collapses in 16 picoseconds (\(t=a_0^3/4r_e^2c=1.556\times10^{-11}\) s) ── one of the reasons quantum mechanics was needed.

And squaring the second derivative gives \(\omega^4\) ── Rayleigh scattering. Blue scatters 4.4 times more than red. Blue sky and red sunset are two sides of one formula.

The radiation spectrum is "the orbit's spectrum tilted by \(+12\) dB/oct", and bremsstrahlung is broadband by Episode 9's bandwidth theorem (stop in \(10^{-18}\) s and you get 329 keV of bandwidth ── X-rays).

And at third order, pre-acceleration appears and causality breaks.
── The most concrete evidence for Episode 2's "the order has a ceiling".

On the numbers: the Larmor formula is non-relativistic and must be replaced by the Liénard form as \(v\to c\) ── the LHC proton entry uses the non-relativistic formula as a reference value (real synchrotron radiation is \(\gamma^4\) larger, and for protons \(\gamma\) is small enough that it is far weaker than for electrons). The classical fall time \(t=a_0^3/(4r_e^2c)\) is the standard result under the quasi-static circular-orbit approximation. Rayleigh's \(1/\lambda^4\) applies when the scatterer is much smaller than the wavelength; aerosols and droplets give Mie scattering with much weaker wavelength dependence (which is why an overcast sky is white). Sky colour also involves atmospheric absorption, ozone and multiple scattering, so it is not determined by \(\omega^4\) alone.
