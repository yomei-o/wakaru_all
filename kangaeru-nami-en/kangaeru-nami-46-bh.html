Exercises

  1. Is §05's "1.5 milliseconds" evidence that information was lost?
    Show answer
    No — it is an estimate of how hard it is to measure. Exponential reddening never makes the amplitude zero; mathematically the information is still there.
    ★ The issue is that observing even one period would take longer than the age of the universe, which is impracticable, not impossible.
    ★★ In practice, though, an exponentially shrinking signal eventually falls below one photon — past that point "cannot be measured" approaches "does not exist" arbitrarily closely. ★ Episode 9's bandwidth theorem and Episode 21's limits on information take over there.
  2. §02 found a black hole's entropy 19 orders above a star's. What does that mean?
    Show answer
    That gravity possesses the degrees of freedom to be maximally disordered. At the same mass and volume, the gravitationally collapsed state has overwhelmingly more microstates.
    ★ By the second law, matter left alone wants to become a black hole — and it does (gravity is the one force that grows more disordered as it clumps).
    ★★ And since \(S\propto M^2\), merging two of them quadruples rather than doubles \(S\) — mergers always increase entropy. ★ Episode 21 said information has a price; the cheapest place to dump it is a horizon.
  3. §01 said black holes heavier than \(4.5\times10^{22}\) kg grow. What does that mean for the universe today?
    Show answer
    That stellar black holes are not evaporating now. Against their \(6\times10^{-8}\) K, the surroundings are at 2.725 K — absorption wins by an enormous margin.
    ★ Evaporation can only begin once the universe has cooled to \(6\times10^{-8}\) K, which requires a further redshift of \(\sim10^{8}\) — some \(10^{17}\) years away at least.
    ★★ That is negligible next to §03's \(10^{67}\)-year lifetime, but "not evaporating now" matters — the only thing worth searching for observationally is the small \(\sim10^{11}\) kg objects from the early universe.

Summary — a horizon is a machine for lowering frequency exponentially

The Hawking temperature is Episode 45's Unruh temperature with \(a\) replaced by the surface gravity \(\kappa=c^4/4GM\) — where there is a horizon, time near it goes logarithmic. That is all. ★ The mass in equilibrium with 2.725 K is \(4.50\times10^{22}\) kg (0.61 lunar masses), so everything heavier is still growing.

The entropy is area ÷ Planck area (\(1.05\times10^{77}\) for a solar mass, by two agreeing calculations) — Episode 39's holography returns, now with a concrete \(\varepsilon\): the horizon. ★ 19 orders above a star of the same mass — gravity is the most disordered configuration.

The evaporation time goes as \(M^3\), and exactly the age of the universe corresponds to \(1.73\times10^{11}\) kg. Negative heat capacity accelerates the ending: the last second releases \(2.05\times10^{22}\) J (some \(3.3\times10^8\) Hiroshima bombs).

The Page curve peaks at \(M=M_0/\sqrt2\), i.e. at 64.6 % of the lifetime (\(1-2^{-3/2}\)) — ★ but that is a model assuming unitarity, not something derived here.

★★★ The core — near the horizon, \(\omega(t)=\omega_0e^{-\kappa t/c}\).
Visible light needs 74.5 e-folds to fall below the inverse age of the universe,
which for a solar mass is a mere 1.5 milliseconds.

★ Episode 5 wrote that "a period longer than the age of the universe is indistinguishable from a constant." A horizon is the machine that sweeps information into that region in 1.5 milliseconds. "No hair" says the same thing differently — nothing vanished; only three components survive in the outside band.

★ Honestly: this episode is translation. The information paradox is not solved, and the gap between "unmeasurable" and "non-existent" is not closed. ★ The one contribution is putting a number on how fast it becomes unmeasurable: 1.5 milliseconds.
