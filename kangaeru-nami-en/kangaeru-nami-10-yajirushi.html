The arrow of time lives
in the parity of the derivative order Under \(t\to-t\), \(d/dt\to-d/dt\), so an \(n\)-th derivative picks up \((-1)^n\).
That alone classifies reversible from irreversible ── the dissipative term was always the odd one.
And fractional orders live continuously in between, as quantities that are actually measured.

01What happens to a derivative under time reversal

Just one line $$t\to-t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{d}{dt}\to-\frac{d}{dt}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{d^n}{dt^n}\to(-1)^n\frac{d^n}{dt^n}$$

Even orders are invariant, odd orders flip. That alone classifies reversible from irreversible.

Conclusion of §02

Only with \(b=0\) does it return to exactly 1.000000.
With friction, flipping the velocity is not enough ── to return you would also have to flip the sign of the friction, which is a different physics.

03There are exactly two routes to reversibility

Order alone is not enough. The imaginary unit \(i\) matters too.

EquationTime derivativeOrder\(i\)?T-symmetry
Wave equation\(\partial^2u/\partial t^2\)2no○ reversible
Diffusion equation\(\partial u/\partial t\)1no× irreversible
Schrödinger\(i\hbar\,\partial\psi/\partial t\)1★ yes○ reversible
Damped oscillator (friction term)\(b\dot x\)1no× irreversible
Maxwell\(\partial\mathbf{E}/\partial t\)1no○ reversible (as a pair)

The heart of §03 ── exactly two routes

① be of even order (the wave equation)
② be of odd order but carry an \(i\) (Schrödinger)

How ② works: \(t\to-t\) sends \(i\hbar\partial\psi/\partial t\to-i\hbar\partial\psi/\partial t\), but taking \(\psi\to\psi^*\) at the same time flips the sign a second time and restores it.
★ Quantum mechanics is reversible thanks to the \(i\).

Maxwell's equations are first order too, but \(\mathbf{E}\) and \(\mathbf{B}\) come as a pair and \(\mathbf{B}\to-\mathbf{B}\) restores invariance ── the same mechanism in another guise.

04So what about fractional orders ── the main point

A non-integer \(\alpha\) is neither even nor odd. As measured in Episode 1, the phase of \((i\omega)^\alpha\) is \(90\alpha\) degrees.

The heart of §04

Fractional orders live continuously between reversible and irreversible.
And \(\tan\delta\) (the loss tangent) is a quantity genuinely measured in viscoelasticity.

Materials line up along the axis of derivative order

Fused silica sits at \(\alpha\approx6\times10^{-6}\) ── almost perfectly "even-order" (conservative), which is why it is used for resonators and tuning forks.
Asphalt sits at \(\alpha\approx1/2\) ── exactly halfway between reversible and irreversible, so it eats vibration.
★ Choosing a material is choosing a derivative order.

Conclusion of §06

Strength of the arrow of time ＝ \(\sin(\alpha\pi/2)\)

Zero at \(\alpha=0\), maximal at \(\alpha=1\), \(1/\sqrt2\) of that at \(\alpha=1/2\).
"How irreversible" is written as a trigonometric function of the order.

07[Hypothesis] Why does physics stay in \(\alpha\in[0,2]\)?

§07 ── as a hypothesis

In Episode 2 we saw that physics keeps to \(\alpha\in[0,2]\) (lower bound at the diffusion boundary \(-1/2\), upper bound from the Ostrogradsky instability).

[Hypothesis] Perhaps that range is exactly the half-period over which the arrow of time goes \(0\to1\to0\).
For \(\alpha>2\), \(\sin(\alpha\pi/2)<0\) ── a backward arrow ── and the sign changes at the same \(\alpha=2\) as the Ostrogradsky instability.

※ This is our conjecture. That the two coincide at \(\alpha=2\) is suggestive, but we give no rigorous argument connecting them.

Exercises

  1. What is wrong with "even a system with friction can be time-reversed in its equation"?
    Show answer
    Substituting \(t\to-t\) gives \(m\ddot x-b\dot x+kx=0\), which is a system with negative friction ── i.e. different physics.
    Time-reversal symmetry means going back with the same equation. The numerical experiment in §02 is exactly that: with \(b\ne0\), flipping the velocity does not return you.
  2. Why is the diffusion equation irreversible while Schrödinger's is reversible?
    Show answer
    Both are first order in time, but Schrödinger's has an \(i\).
    Doing \(t\to-t\) and \(\psi\to\psi^*\) together, the sign flip of \(i\) cancels the sign flip of the time derivative (anti-unitary time reversal).
    The diffusion equation has no \(i\), hence no escape route ── and indeed solving it backwards blows up numerically.
  3. What \(\alpha\) do you want in a vibration-damping rubber?
    Show answer
    As large as possible (you want to eat vibrational energy). But \(\alpha\to1\) is a pure dashpot and cannot support a static load.
    Practical damping materials sit around \(\alpha\approx0.3\) because that is the compromise between supporting (\(\alpha=0\)-like) and absorbing (\(\alpha=1\)-like).
    Resonators — tuning forks, quartz, LIGO's suspension fibres — push \(\alpha\) as low as it will go. Design is choosing a position on this axis.

Summary　The arrow of time was written in the order

Under \(t\to-t\), an \(n\)-th derivative picks up \((-1)^n\). Even orders are invariant, odd orders flip ── that alone classifies reversible from irreversible. In \(m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=0\) there is exactly one odd-order term, and it is the only dissipative one (confirmed numerically: only \(b=0\) returns to 1.000000).

There are exactly two routes to reversibility ── be even-order, or be odd-order with an \(i\). Quantum mechanics is reversible thanks to the \(i\); the diffusion equation has no such escape.

And fractional orders live continuously in between: \(\tan\delta=\tan(\alpha\pi/2)\), a quantity actually measured in viscoelasticity. Fused silica sits at \(\alpha\approx6\times10^{-6}\), asphalt at exactly \(\alpha=1/2\). Choosing a material is choosing a derivative order.

Dissipation proportional to \(\sin(\alpha\pi/2)\) was confirmed numerically (agreement to \(10^{-14}\)). "How irreversible" is a trigonometric function of the order.

Strength of the arrow of time ＝ \(\sin(\alpha\pi/2)\).
And physics' range \(\alpha\in[0,2]\) is where the arrow makes one round trip (hypothesis).

An "effective order \(\alpha\)" can be read uniquely from \(\tan\delta\) only if the material is describable by a single springpot.
