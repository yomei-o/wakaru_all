Thinking in Waves Episode 44 / Part XV — Making the Medium Ourselves

Designing the dispersion relation

Metamaterials —
Periodic in space gives a gap in frequency; periodic in time gives a gap in momentum Until now the medium was given. From here we make it.
★ What fixes the sign of a negative index is the presence of loss — the causality condition \(\mathrm{Im}\,n>0\) (Episode 8).
★★ And make the structure periodic in time and the gap opens in \(k\), where the wave grows (rate 0.024993, against the prediction \(hck/4=0.025\)).

★ Now we invert it — choose the dispersion relation first, then build a structure that realizes it. But this series has already decided what may be built.

01Negative index — the sign is fixed by loss

With \(n^2=\varepsilon\mu\) and both \(\varepsilon,\mu\) negative, \(n^2>0\) — two signs are available. The one that is chosen is the one that decays when loss is added (\(\mathrm{Im}\,n>0\)).

The point of §01

★★ Only \(\varepsilon<0\) and \(\mu<0\) gives \(\mathrm{Re}\,n<0\). With just one negative, \(n\) is almost purely imaginary — Episode 28's evanescent wave (\(k^2<0\), cannot travel).
★ The sign was chosen by loss. In a lossless ideal medium the two signs are mathematically indistinguishable. Admit even an infinitesimal loss and the decaying branch is unique.
★★ Episode 8's causality is doing the job of picking a sign.

The point of §02

\(d(\omega\varepsilon)/d\omega=1+\omega_p^2/\omega^2\) is always positive — dispersion is what lets a negative \(\varepsilon\) keep a positive energy.
★★ Conversely, "a negative \(\varepsilon\) without dispersion" cannot exist.
★ That is why a negative-index medium is always narrowband — make \(\varepsilon<0\) over a wide band and \(d(\omega\varepsilon)/d\omega\) must go negative somewhere.

Exercises

  1. §06 found the wave growing inside the momentum gap. Where does the energy come from?
    Show answer
    From whatever is wobbling \(\varepsilon(t)\). Changing the permittivity takes work, and that work goes into the wave.
    ★ Exactly Episode 40's swing — the person pumping the swing is \(\varepsilon(t)\).
    ★★ So passivity (Episodes 17–18) is not violated — the system as a whole is externally driven. ★ When you hear "a metamaterial amplified the wave," the correct first question is where the power is being fed in.
  2. §03's sum rule fixes a total. What, then, does negative index pay with?
    Show answer
    Bandwidth and loss. By §02, \(\varepsilon<0\) always comes with dispersion, and strong dispersion means a narrow band. And strong dispersion means, by Kramers–Kronig, a large \(\mathrm{Im}\,\varepsilon\) nearby — that is, large loss.
    ★ "Negative index is narrowband and lossy" is not an experimental inconvenience but a consequence of causality.
    ★★ It is Episode 8's "real and imaginary parts cannot be designed independently" in action. ★ So a design programme aiming at "lossless broadband negative index" is a dead end in principle.
  3. §04 called the inside of a gap "evanescent." How does that differ from Episode 41's topological gap?
    Show answer
    The gap itself is the same; what differs is what sits at the edge. Both are bands in which the bulk cannot propagate.
    ★ But in Episode 41 the bulk integer (winding, Chern) is non-zero, so states must appear at the boundary (bulk–boundary correspondence). In an ordinary photonic crystal the integer is zero and nothing appears.
    ★★ So §04 suffices if you only want a gap, but to get a one-way road at the edge the integer must be non-zero — which requires breaking time-reversal symmetry (a magnetic field, or time modulation as in §06). ★ §06 is also the doorway to that.

Summary — a metamaterial is a designed dispersion relation

The sign of a negative index is fixed by loss. Only \(\varepsilon<0\) and \(\mu<0\) gives \(\mathrm{Re}\,n<0\); with one alone, \(n\) is almost purely imaginary — Episode 28's evanescent wave. In a lossless ideal medium the two signs cannot be told apart.

And it cannot be built without dispersion. The energy condition is \(d(\omega\varepsilon)/d\omega>0\), and for Drude that is \(1+\omega_p^2/\omega^2\), always positive — dispersion is what permits \(\varepsilon<0\). ★ Hence negative index is always narrowband.

And the sum rule \(\int\omega\,\mathrm{Im}\,\varepsilon\,d\omega=(\pi/2)\omega_p^2\) (numerically 1.57077 against 1.57080) — the right side depends on neither the position nor the sharpness of the resonance. A designer controls the allocation, never the total.

In a 1D photonic crystal, \(|\mathrm{tr}\,M/2|>1\) is the gap; at index ratio 2 its relative width is 0.376. At the band edge \(v_g/c\) falls 0.628 → 0.017, a factor of 37 (slow light). But stretching the delay 14× moved the delay–bandwidth product by only a factor of two (★ and not monotonically — calling it constant would overstate it).

★★★ The core —
periodic in space: a gap in frequency, and inside it the wave decays.
Periodic in time: a gap in momentum, and inside it the wave grows.

The time-modulated equation is Episode 40's Mathieu equation exactly: the gap is centred at \(k=\Omega/2c\), the growth rate is 0.024993 (prediction \(hck/4=0.025\)), and \(\det M=1.000000\). ★ Decay or growth is just which direction you cannot travel in — cannot travel in space and you shrink with distance; cannot travel in time and, since time cannot be refused, you grow.

★ And what may be built is decided by three gatekeepers — passivity (Ep. 17–18), Ostrogradsky (Ep. 29), and the sum rule (Ep. 8). Negative index breaks none of them, which is why it exists. The perfect lens is hard because the loss cannot be removed — and that loss is what fixed the sign of \(n\) in the first place.

Negative refractive index (Veselago, Pendry), branch selection via \(\mathrm{Im}\,n>0\), the energy-density condition in a dispersive medium, the f-sum rule, transfer matrices and band gaps in 1D photonic crystals, the delay–bandwidth product, and momentum gaps with amplification in time-modulated media (photonic time crystals) are all well-known results and none are discoveries of this article — what this article did was gather them under the single question "what can and cannot be built," and set the space-periodic and time-periodic cases side by side. The delay–bandwidth product in §05 is not constant: in this model and these parameters a 16-fold change in \(\gamma\) keeps it within a factor of two, but it is not monotonic (smallest near \(\gamma\approx0.1\)) and it is not a universal constant. §04 and §06 are minimal one-dimensional, two-component models and are a different problem from designing a real three-dimensional device (realizing isotropic \(\mu<0\) in particular carries difficulties not treated here).
