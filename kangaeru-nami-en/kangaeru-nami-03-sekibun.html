Thinking in Waves Episode 3 ／ Part II · Constants as waves

From one line of code to the physical constants

Is the constant of integration
the physical constant? ── what lives at \(\omega=0\) Integration is \(\times1/(i\omega)\). It diverges at \(\omega=0\), so the DC component alone is undetermined.
That is what the "constant of integration" really is ── zero frequency, a component of infinite period.
And with only an observation window \(T\), we cannot tell a wave of period \(>T\) from a constant.

Fractional derivative by the spectral method (excerpt) $$\texttt{if (kk === 0) \{ re[k]=0; im[k]=0; continue; \}}$$

★ DC cannot be defined

The heart of §01

The constant of integration was the zero-frequency component.

Differentiation crushes DC, so it throws information away. The inverse operation therefore cannot restore what was thrown away ── and the remainder is written "\(+C\)".
That \(C\) you were told never to forget in school is the origin of the spectrum.

02What is zero frequency?

\(\omega=0\) means infinite period. No real observation can measure that. All we can measure are periods shorter than our window.

Fourier resolution $$\Delta f=\frac{1}{T}\qquad(T=\text{length observed})$$

A wave with period longer than \(T\) never completes a single cycle ── indistinguishable from a constant

Conclusion of §02 ── rewriting the definition of a constant

A physical constant ＝ a component whose period exceeds the age of the universe

It is not that it "does not change" but that we have no means of observing a change.
So to the question "are constants integrals?" one can answer: yes — but the interval of integration is the history of the universe, and anything slower than that is invisible in principle.

03Some things this view explains for free

①
A conserved quantity is a DC component\(dQ/dt=0\) is the same statement as "the spectrum of \(Q\) lives only at \(\omega=0\)"
②
The line between "law" and "initial condition"a law is a relation among \(\omega\ne0\); an initial condition is the value at \(\omega=0\). The constant of integration is precisely an initial condition
③
The choice of units also lives at DCchanging the length of the metre changes no physics ── because it is a change of DC

③ sets up the next episode

When we ask "what happens if we differentiate a constant", differentiating a quantity that is merely a convention about units gives nothing but zero.
And the speed of light \(c\) is exactly such a quantity ── it has been a defined value since 1983.
That is where we start next time.

The heart of §04 ── there are two kinds

Constants of integration come in two kinds: those that show up in observations, and those that do not.

The ones that do not (\(V_0\), gauge) become symmetries ── because changing them does nothing.
The ones that do (\(\Lambda\)) become new constants ── because they must be measured.

★ The cosmological constant \(\Lambda\) is literally a case of "a constant of integration becoming physics". Einstein noticed this freedom when writing the equations, discarded it once, and observation later brought it back.

05So can \(\omega=0\) be measured at all?

No. And it is not a matter of technology but of definition.

Why it is a matter of principle $$\text{"the }\omega=0\text{ component of }\alpha\text{"}\ =\ \text{"the value of }\alpha\text{ itself"}$$

Constraining it means saying "\(\alpha\) is not 1/137",
which is a statement about measurement error, not about detecting change

Conclusion of this episode

\(\omega=0\) is not a thing to be measured; it is the value itself.
So the question "is a constant changing?" must always be translated into a search for components at \(\omega\ne0\).
And the smaller \(\omega\), the worse the experimental sensitivity ── next time we measure that "sensitivity spectrum".

Exercises

  1. In what sense does differentiation throw information away?
    Show answer
    \(\times(i\omega)\) multiplies by zero at \(\omega=0\), so the DC component vanishes completely.
    Just as multiplying by zero cannot be undone, this is an irreversible operation. Hence the inverse (integration) always leaves an undetermined constant.
  2. Why does the zero of potential \(V_0\) not appear in physics while the cosmological constant \(\Lambda\) does?
    Show answer
    Because gravity couples to energy itself.
    In Newtonian mechanics the force is \(-dV/dx\), so \(V_0\) cancels; in general relativity the vacuum energy density curves spacetime. The symmetry "nothing happens if you shift the origin" fails for gravity.
    This is also the doorway to the cosmological constant problem (calculated and observed vacuum energy differ by \(10^{120}\)).
  3. Give an everyday example of "a period longer than the window looks constant".
    Show answer
    Continental drift. Over a human lifetime (resolving down to \(4\times10^{-10}\) Hz) the continents' positions look constant.
    Through the geological window (\(10^{-15}\) Hz) they are obviously moving. Whether something is "constant" is not a property of the object but of its relation to the observation window.
    There is no argument that the physical constants must be exempt from the same logic.

Summary　What lives at \(\omega=0\)

Integration is \(\times1/(i\omega)\), which diverges at \(\omega=0\) and cannot fix the DC component. That is what the constant of integration "\(+C\)" really is ── the origin of the spectrum.

And \(\omega=0\) means infinite period. With only a window \(T\), we cannot distinguish a wave of period \(>T\) from a constant. Even using the age of the universe, \(2.3\times10^{-18}\) Hz is the floor.

Several things then connect for free ── conserved quantities are DC components; the boundary between law and initial condition is the boundary between \(\omega\ne0\) and \(\omega=0\); the choice of units lives at DC too.

Constants of integration come in two kinds. Those invisible to observation (the zero of potential, gauge) become symmetries; those visible (the cosmological constant \(\Lambda\)) become new constants. \(\Lambda\) is literally a constant of integration that became physics.

A physical constant ＝ a component whose period exceeds the age of the universe.
Not unchanging — merely beyond any means of observing change.
