Thinking in Waves Episode 37 ／ Part XIII · Frontier physics as waves

Breaking the assumption of a clean medium

Anderson localization ──
whether a wave stops depends on whether it comes back In a disordered medium a wave stops with no absorption at all. In one dimension any disorder, however weak, localizes (\(\xi\propto W^{-2}\); measured slope −1.97).
★★ And the reason it depends on dimension came out ── a wave that never returns cannot interfere with itself.
★ The dimensional dependence of localization is Pólya's recurrence theorem.

Prerequisites: Episodes 5, 6, 8, 27, 28, 30Computation: kenshou/kyokuzai.js

For 36 episodes the medium has been clean and uniform. Now we break that.
The naive expectation is "it scatters, so it diffuses" ── but past a threshold it does not even diffuse (Anderson localization, 1958).
★ This is not restricted to quantum systems ── light, sound, microwaves, water waves, cold atoms: it is a pure wave phenomenon.

The heart of §01

★ So experiments look "not localized" ── the sample is simply too short. The same structure as Episode 5's "a period longer than the age of the universe is indistinguishable from a constant".

03★★ Why it depends on dimension ── does the wave come back?

Behaviour changes with dimension ── in 1D and 2D any disorder localizes; in 3D weak disorder still diffuses (there is a mobility edge).
★ Why is 2 the boundary? Localization happens because the wave returns and interferes with itself, so what matters is how often it returns.

The heart of §03 ── ★★★ Pólya's theorem (1921)

\(d=1\) and \(d=2\) keep growing as the grid is refined (divergent). Only \(d=3\) settles (Watson's integral 1.516386…; it approaches only as \(1/N\), so convergence is slow).

★ A random walk in \(d\le2\) always returns to the origin (recurrent).
In \(d\ge3\) it may never return ── in 3D the return probability is \(1-1/1.516386=0.340537\).

★★ That is where the dimensional dependence of localization comes from.
A wave that never returns cannot interfere with itself, so it does not localize. In \(d\le2\) it always returns, so the interference piles up and localization is inevitable. In 3D only 34 % return ── not enough unless the disorder is strong.

\(d=1\) grows as \(\sqrt{\tau_\phi}\), \(d=2\) as \(\ln\tau_\phi\) (0.3665 per two decades), and \(d=3\) stops.

Exercises

  1. If 1D always localizes, why does current flow in a copper wire?
    Show the answer
    Three reasons. ① A wire is not one-dimensional (tens of millions of transverse channels ＝ effectively 3D). ② The localization length exceeds the sample ── by §01, \(\xi\propto W^{-2}\), astronomically long in a clean metal. ③ ★ Phase is not preserved ── localization is an interference effect, so phonon and electron–electron scattering that destroys phase destroys it.
    ★ ③ dominates in practice, which is why localization experiments are done cold ── to stop the phase-breaking scattering.
    ★★ This is Episode 22's decoherence exactly: if the phase-coherence length \(L_\phi\) is shorter than \(\xi\), localization is invisible.
  2. §02 showed the exponent fluctuates. What does that mean for measurement?
    Show the answer
    One sample does not give a representative value. The right thing to average is \(\ln\) of the conductance, not the conductance (the distribution is log-normal).
    ★ Turned around ── the size of the fluctuation itself carries information. It is called universal conductance fluctuation, and is of order \(e^2/h\) regardless of sample details.
    ★★ "Look at the distribution, not the mean" is the practical difference between a statistical and a deterministic exponent (the same habit as the scatter of the 1/f exponent \(\beta\) in Episode 27).
  3. Is §03's "does it return" related to Episode 30's "does it leave a tail"?
    Show the answer
    Not directly ── they are different questions. Episode 30 asked what remains behind the front in a clean medium (Huygens). This episode asks whether a wave keeps spreading in a disordered one.
    ★ They do share something ── in both, the boundary is where a logarithm appears at \(d=2\) (§05).
    ★★ The caution this series keeps repeating: when you see the same number (here \(d=2\)), first check which integral diverged. Same number, different integral, different phenomenon.

Summary　Whether it stops depends on whether it comes back

Disorder a medium and the wave stops with no absorption at all (Anderson localization).

★ In one dimension any disorder localizes. Measuring the Lyapunov exponent by transfer matrix gives \(\xi W^2\) roughly constant (86–94) and a slope of −1.97 for \(\ln\xi\) against \(\ln W\) (prediction −2). But \(\xi\approx8.6\times10^7\) sites at \(W=0.001\) ── experiments miss it because samples are short (Episode 5's structure again).

The same exponential decay has a different cause ── an evanescent wave's \(k^2<0\) exponent is deterministic and does not fluctuate, while localization is a statistical exponent from multiple-scattering interference and varies by 2.5 % between samples.

★★★ And the reason for the dimensional dependence came out ── Pólya's recurrence theorem.
A random walk in \(d\le2\) always returns; in 3D the return probability is 0.340537.
A wave that never returns cannot interfere with itself, so \(d\le2\) always localizes while 3D needs strong disorder.

The weak-localization correction says the same thing ── \(d=1\) grows as \(\sqrt{\tau_\phi}\), \(d=2\) as \(\ln\tau_\phi\), \(d=3\) converges. In \(d\le2\) the correction diverges and the diffusion picture itself breaks.

★ Episode 30 also singled out \(d=2\), but on checking it is a different origin (geometry versus interference) ── though with a common root: in both, it is the dimension where the power becomes \(-1\) and the integral turns logarithmic. The same mathematics as Episode 27's 1/f.

Finally ── none of this needs quantum mechanics. It happens with light, sound and microwaves. \(\hbar\) is stage machinery; being a wave is what matters (Episode 8's conclusion again).
