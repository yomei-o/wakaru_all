The heart of §01

mass → speed → mass

Orders 0 and 2 are both called "mass". One word, two different features of a curve.

Written out, the definition of effective mass is ──

$$\frac{1}{m^*}=\frac{1}{\hbar^2}\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}$$

★ The reciprocal of mass is the curvature of the dispersion relation.

Conclusion of §02

Up to \(v/c=0.1\), order 2 alone is good to \(10^{-5}\).
Ordinary physics gets away with "mass is a constant" because the parabolic approximation works so well.

★ Conversely ── effective mass stops being constant when you leave the parabola.
In semiconductors that actually happens at high fields (non-parabolicity).

03Electrons in a solid ── effective mass is "reluctance to hop"

The simplest solid (tight binding) $$E(k)=-2t\cos(ka)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E\approx-2t+ta^2k^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad m^*=\frac{\hbar^2}{2ta^2}$$

\(t\): hopping strength to a neighbour; \(a\): lattice spacing
★ The easier the hop, the lighter ── effective mass is just "reluctance to move"

§03 ── at the top of the band, mass goes negative

Expand the same formula at \(k=\pi/a\) (the band top):

band bottom \(k=0\): \(m^*/m_e=+0.4233\)　　／　　band top \(k=\pi/a\): \(m^*/m_e=-0.4233\)

★ Push it and it goes backwards ── that is a hole. Semiconductor engineering's "p-type" is a name for this negative curvature.

In Episode 10 it was "the parity of the order sets the arrow of time". Here ── the sign of the second derivative sets the direction of the force.

05Graphene ── zero mass and a light speed of \(c/300\)

A cone at the origin $$E=\pm\hbar v_F|k|,\qquad v_F\approx1.0\times10^6\ \text{m/s}=\frac{c}{300}$$

★ The dispersion relation of a massless particle (\(E=pc\) with \(c\) replaced by \(v_F\))

Something interesting happens. Compute this world's fine-structure constant ──

A slower light speed means stronger coupling $$\alpha_{\text{graphene}}=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar v_F}=\alpha\cdot\frac{c}{v_F}=0.00729735\times299.8=\mathbf{2.19}$$

The heart of §05 ── strongly coupled QED on a benchtop

\(\alpha_{\text{graphene}}=2.2\) ── greater than one.
In Episode 5 the vacuum's \(\alpha\) ran between 1/137 and 1/129. Here it is 2.2 ── ★ a non-perturbative regime sitting on a desk.

The reason is visible in the formula ── \(\alpha\propto1/c\), so dividing the effective light speed by 300 multiplies the coupling by 300.
Where light is slow, electromagnetism is strong.

Massless, and yet a cyclotron mass is definable ── \(m_c=E_F/v_F^2\).

06Giving the photon a mass ── inside a superconductor

Episode 6 said "mass = cutoff frequency". Run it backwards ── manufacture a cutoff and the photon grows a mass.

The Meissner effect is evanescent decay $$\lambda_L=\frac{\hbar}{m_\gamma c}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad m_\gamma=\frac{\hbar}{\lambda_L c}$$

The heart of §06 ── the Higgs mechanism in miniature

Inside niobium, the photon has a mass of 5.06 eV.

This is the Higgs mechanism, scaled down ── a superconducting condensate gives the photon mass; the Higgs field gives the W and Z theirs. The same single formula.

Check it:

W boson: mass 80.377 GeV, range \(\hbar/(M_Wc)=2.455\times10^{-18}\) m
Photon in niobium: mass 5.06 eV, range \(3.900\times10^{-8}\) m

Mass ratio \(1.588\times10^{10}\)　／　length ratio \(1.589\times10^{10}\)　／　ratio of ratios \(=0.999934\)

★ Confirmation that "the weak force is weak" and "a superconductor expels magnetic field" are bound by one formula.

07Is the photon in vacuum really massless?

Translating the bound into a cutoff frequency $$m_\gamma<10^{-18}\ \text{eV}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_c<2.42\times10^{-4}\ \text{Hz}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{period}>69\ \text{minutes}$$ $$\text{range}\ \lambda=c/\omega_c>1.97\times10^{11}\ \text{m}=1.3\ \text{AU}$$

§07 ── back to Episodes 3 and 4

The vacuum is "possibly a waveguide with a cutoff somewhere beyond a period of an hour". Nothing more can be said.

★ That is ── "the photon is massless" is the claim that the cutoff frequency lies below the observation window.
Exactly the structure of Episode 3's "a physical constant = a component with period longer than the window". The low-frequency side is always "not yet visible".

Conclusion of §08

"Mass" is not one quantity but a name for a way of reading a dispersion relation.

Read the intercept and you get rest mass; the curvature, effective mass; the decay length, a photon mass in a medium. All features of the same curve \(\omega(k)\).

Exercises

  1. In a heavy-fermion system the electron mass is 200×. Did the electron get heavier?
    Show answer
    No. The electron is the same; what changed is how the dispersion relation bends.
    When \(f\) electrons mix strongly with conduction electrons, the band becomes extremely flat near the Fermi level ── a flat band means small curvature means large effective mass.
    ★ In §03's language, it is "hard to hop": the \(t\) in \(m^*=\hbar^2/(2ta^2)\) is small. What is heavy is not the electron but the environment it sits in.
  2. What does \(\alpha=2.2\) in graphene mean, in Episode 5's terms?
    Show answer
    In Episode 5, \(\alpha\) ran with \(\ln E\), from 1/137 to 1/129 in vacuum ── always far below one, so perturbation theory works.
    In graphene \(\alpha_g=\alpha\,c/v_F=2.2\), so the expansion parameter exceeds one; summing Feynman diagrams does not converge.
    ★ In real graphene, though, screening reduces the effective \(\alpha_g\) below one, and whether it is truly strongly coupled is debated. What we computed is the bare, unscreened value.
  3. How could "the photon is massless" be falsified?
    Show answer
    By finding a cutoff frequency. Concretely ──
    ① look for a frequency below which electromagnetic waves stop propagating (a vacuum waveguide cutoff)
    ② look for a distance at which Coulomb's law departs from \(1/r^2\) (a Yukawa deviation)
    ③ check whether planetary or galactic magnetic fields decay faster than expected
    ★ All three are one direction: go to lower frequency / longer distance ── a concrete form of Episode 3's "\(\omega=0\) is out of reach". So the bound can keep falling, but zero can never be proved.

Summary　mass → speed → mass

Expanding the dispersion relation at \(k=0\) gives rest mass at order 0, group velocity at order 1, and the reciprocal effective mass at order 2. One word pointing at different features of one curve.

Checked against relativity, order 2 alone is good to \(10^{-5}\) up to \(v/c=0.1\) ── "mass is a constant" works because the parabolic approximation is so good.

In solids, \(m^*=\hbar^2/(2ta^2)\). The easier the hop the lighter, and at the top of a band it goes negative (holes). Real materials span 0.014 to 200 ── four orders; mass is a design parameter.

Graphene is a world of zero mass and effective light speed \(c/300\), where \(\alpha_{\text{graphene}}=2.19\) ── where light is slow, electromagnetism is strong.

Inside a superconductor the photon grows a mass (5.06 eV in niobium). Against the W boson, the mass ratio and length ratio agree to \(10^{-4}\) ── "the weak force is weak" and "magnetic field is expelled" are one formula.

★ The reciprocal of mass is the curvature of the dispersion relation.
And "the photon is massless" is the claim that its cutoff lies below the observation window.
