Exercises

  1. Where do the “2.2 decades” of §02 come from?
    Show answer
    From the width of a single Lorentzian’s bend. One Lorentzian \(\tau/(1+\omega^2\tau^2)\) takes about a decade on each side of \(\omega\tau=1\) to turn its slope from 0 to \(-2\) (the same structure as the “5.36 decades each” measured in Episode 48 §03).
    ★ At the ends of the superposition, those not-yet-fully-bent Lorentzians are what you see.
    ★★ So the 2.2 is “1.1 decades at each end” ── about the width of one Lorentzian’s bend.
    ★ Loosen the criterion (to \(\pm0.1\)) and the 2.2 shrinks ── the 2.2 here is the value under a \(\pm0.05\) criterion. ★ As Episode 55 insisted, the criterion is part of the number and should be written with it.
  2. §04 says “no cutoff is not measurable.” Was Episode 2’s claim therefore empty?
    Show answer
    No ── Episode 2 did not claim “no cutoff.” What it said was “\(1/f\) cannot be made from a single time constant; it takes many superposed,” and that holds over the decades actually observed (§02: if you saw 6 decades, \(\tau\) spans 8.2).
    ★ What cannot be measured is only “does it continue beyond that” ── the substance of the claim is intact.
    ★★ In the handbook’s phrasing from Episode 49 ── write “\(1/f\) holds over \(N\) decades,” never “it holds forever.”
    ★ This series never wrote “forever” in Episode 2 ── but it did not state the number of decades either, and that is what this episode fixes.
  3. §05 concludes that changing the temperature is strongest. Which of Episode 54’s ways of buying range is that?
    Show answer
    ③ change the system itself. Changing \(T\) shifts \(\tau=\tau_0e^{E/kT}\) bodily, so the window of visible \(\tau\) moves with the same sample and the same instrument.
    ★ And it moves exponentially ── at \(E=1.0\) eV, 300 K → 200 K takes \(\tau/\tau_0\) from \(6\times10^{16}\) to \(1.6\times10^{25}\), more than 8 decades.
    ★★ Episode 54 found that raising \(Re\) only helps as \(\log Re\); here the dependence is exponential, so the leverage is in a different league.
    ★★★ In other words, the very mechanism called “activation” hands us a way to buy range ── Episode 28’s “addition becomes multiplication,” now working on the measurement side.

Summary — “many” had a price tag

Carrying the tools assembled in Part XVIII, we went back to the \(1/f\) of Episode 2.

First, \(\beta=2-\gamma\) confirmed to \(10^{-6}\) (with \(g(\tau)\propto\tau^{-\gamma}\)). Episode 27’s “\(D(E)\) fixes \(\beta\)” survives, and measuring \(D(E)\) independently would predict \(\beta\) ── it remains falsifiable.

★ Episode 2’s “superposition of many time constants” carried a price ── the decades you want to see, plus 2.2. To say “we saw 6 decades of \(1/f\)” requires \(\tau\) spanning at least 8.2 decades.

★★ And “\(1/f\) has no low-frequency cutoff” is not a measurable claim ── seeing the knee requires an observation longer than \(\tau_{\max}\), and ten years buys only 11.5 decades. It is the same shape as Episode 5’s “a period longer than the age of the universe is indistinguishable from a constant,” and in Episode 55’s words it is “cannot measure,” not “does not exist.”

★ The substance of Episode 2 is intact ── “a single time constant cannot do it” holds over the decades observed. ★ What needs fixing is only the phrasing: write “over \(N\) decades,” not “forever.”

★★ The strongest way to settle it is to change the temperature ── since \(\tau=\tau_0e^{E/kT}\), going from 300 K to 200 K moves \(\tau\) by more than 8 decades. That is Episode 54’s “change the system itself,” and it works exponentially ── far faster than observing longer.
