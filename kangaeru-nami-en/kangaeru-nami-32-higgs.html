What was the negative mass term?

The Higgs as waves ──
the Higgs boson is AM, the Goldstone is FM Flip the sign of the mass term and the cutoff frequency turns imaginary ── the wave does not oscillate, it grows exponentially.
★ The time-domain version of Episode 28's "a wave that cannot travel is an exponential". Nonlinearity stops it ── but does not choose a vacuum. Dissipation does.
★★ What is left are two modes: radial ＝ amplitude modulation, angular ＝ phase modulation. AM and FM, exactly as in audio.

Episode 6 read it this way ── mass ＝ cutoff frequency. Klein–Gordon's \(\omega^2=c^2k^2+(mc^2/\hbar)^2\) was word for word a waveguide.
Which turns the Higgs question into this ── ★ how do you give empty vacuum a cutoff frequency?

01★ A negative mass term ＝ an imaginary cutoff frequency

The Higgs potential has the mass term's sign reversed $$V(\phi)=-\mu^2|\phi|^2+\lambda|\phi|^4\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \omega^2=c^2k^2-\mu^2$$

All we did was put \(m^2=-\mu^2\). For \(k<\mu\), \(\omega^2<0\) ── \(\omega\) is purely imaginary

03★★ The Higgs boson is amplitude modulation, the Goldstone is phase modulation

Look at small fluctuations around the vacuum \(v\). The field is complex, so there are two directions ── \(\phi=(v+h)e^{i\theta}\). \(V\) depends only on \(|\phi|\).

Two directions $$\text{radial}:\ V''(v)=-\mu^2+3\lambda v^2=2\mu^2\ \Rightarrow\ \omega=\sqrt2\,\mu\qquad\qquad \text{angular}:\ V\ \text{is flat}\ \Rightarrow\ \omega=0$$

Wobble the carrier's size, or wobble its phase ── that is the whole difference.

Exercises

  1. A "tachyon" is not a faster-than-light particle. What is it, as a wave?
    Show the answer
    An unstable mode. With \(\omega^2=c^2k^2-\mu^2\) and \(k<\mu\), \(\omega\) is purely imaginary, so \(e^{-i\omega t}\to e^{\mu t/\hbar}\) ── not oscillation but exponential growth.
    ★ Not "too fast" but "does not travel at all, and grows where it stands" ── the time-domain version of Episode 28's below-cutoff evanescent wave (\(e^{-\kappa x}\)).
    ★ Indeed the group velocity \(d\omega/dk\) is imaginary for \(k<\mu\), so the word "speed" loses its meaning.
  2. If the Higgs boson is AM, why does it "have mass"?
    Show the answer
    Because there is a restoring force in the amplitude direction. \(V''(v)=2\mu^2>0\), so displacing the amplitude pulls it back ── and how fast it pulls back is exactly the cutoff frequency ＝ the mass (Episode 6).
    The angular direction has a flat \(V\), no restoring force, hence zero cutoff frequency ＝ zero mass.
    ★ "Mass" is the strength of that mode's restoring force: we are measuring the curvature of the Mexican hat's cross-section, nothing more.
  3. §02 found that without dissipation no vacuum is chosen. Is that a problem in the real universe?
    Show the answer
    No ── the universe supplies friction. Expansion automatically brings Hubble friction \(3H\dot\phi\), and the Higgs field can decay into other particles (it has Yukawa couplings).
    ★ The lesson runs the other way: breaking does not follow from the potential alone ── the potential only offers possible vacua; which one is taken is settled by dissipation.
    ★ This is exactly the structure of inflationary "slow roll" (the friction-dominated regime), and it is why cosmology always writes the \(3H\dot\phi\) term.

Summary　Make the cutoff imaginary, let it grow, then stop it

★ A negative mass term is an imaginary cutoff frequency. With \(\omega^2=c^2k^2-\mu^2\), every mode below \(k=\mu\) grows, fastest at \(k=0\) with time constant \(\hbar/\mu=7.4\times10^{-27}\) s. The time-domain counterpart of Episode 28's spatial exponential.

Numerically the slope of \(\ln\phi\) matched \(\mu=88.5651\) to 6 digits, and the nonlinear turning point is \(\sqrt2\,v=348.2072\) GeV (agreeing to \(1.2\times10^{-10}\)).

★★ But a prediction failed ── the field does not settle into a vacuum. It swings forever between \(\pm\sqrt2v\). Settling needs dissipation; at \(\gamma\ge10\) GeV it converges on \(v\) to \(10^{-7}\). ★ Nonlinearity halts the runaway but does not choose. "Symmetry breaking" requires dissipation ── which is exactly what cosmology's Hubble friction provides.

★★★ What remains are two directions ── radially, a restoring force \(2\mu^2\) giving \(\omega=\sqrt2\mu=m_H\) (matched to \(10^{-4}\)); angularly, no restoring force at all (\(|\phi|\) varying by \(2\times10^{-11}\)). In audio terms, amplitude modulation and phase modulation: by FFT, AM has two sidebands while FM has an unending row following \(J_n(\beta)\) (6-digit agreement).

★ The Higgs boson is AM; the Nambu–Goldstone boson is FM.
And the FM sidebands are absorbed by the gauge carrier as its longitudinal wave (2 → 3 polarizations, the count unchanged).

The vacuum expectation value gives \(m_W\) and \(m_Z\), and the Yukawa couplings \(y=\sqrt2m/v\) put only the top at 0.9919 ≈ 1, a factor \(3.4\times10^5\) above the electron. ── But nothing explains why \(\mu^2<0\), nor \(\lambda\), nor the Yukawa hierarchy, nor \(v=246\) GeV. Seeing it as waves explains the mechanism, not the values (Episode 15).
