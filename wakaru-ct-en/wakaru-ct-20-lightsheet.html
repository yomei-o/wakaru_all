Extra 2 of the previous series ended on this: "if you write the universe as a computer with finite resources, what should be constrained is not \(a(t)\) but the information on the light sheets. Nobody has done that calculation." Extra 3 applied Bousso's covariant entropy bound to the apparent horizon, derived \(s\le3H/4\ell_P^2\), and got as far as showing that saturating it identically gives \(a\propto t^{1/3}\). Today we go further — imposed as a constraint rather than a saturation, does a prediction come out? The answer first: no. And why not is the interesting part.

01Following the bound through time

What is constrained $$s\ \le\ \frac{3H}{4\ell_P^2}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad f\equiv\frac{s}{3H/4\ell_P^2}\ \le\ 1$$

with \(s\propto a^{-3}\) and \(H\propto1/t\)

$$f\ \propto\ t^{\,1-3p}\qquad(a\propto t^p)$$

Take the standard thermal history (radiation \(p=1/2\) → matter \(p=2/3\)), set \(f\approx1\) at the Planck era, and stack up to today.

Today's margin is 33 orders. ── The only epoch actually constrained is the Planck era.

02Saturating at the Planck era was an identity

"The bound saturates exactly at the Planck era" looks meaningful. Let us measure it with Episode 19's tool.

Check by dimensions alone

at the Planck era \(T\sim M_{\rm Pl}\), hence

$$s\sim T^3\sim\frac{1}{\ell_P^3},\qquad H\sim\frac{1}{t_P}\sim\frac{c}{\ell_P}$$

substituting

$$f=\frac{s}{3H/4\ell_P^2}\sim\frac{1/\ell_P^3}{(1/\ell_P)/\ell_P^2}=1$$

Conclusion of §02

\(O(1)\) from dimensional analysis alone. An [identity] in Episode 19's classification — surprise 0 bits.
"The bound saturates at the Planck era" was a check, not a discovery.

After Dirac's large numbers (Episode 7) and the Landauer limit (Episode 10), this is the third "looks meaningful, is an identity". The procedure built last episode went straight to work.

04The universe that stays saturated is stiff

Two lines $$\frac{\sigma}{a^3}=\frac{3\dot a}{4Ga}\quad(\sigma=\text{comoving entropy density, constant})$$ $$\Longrightarrow\quad a^2\dot a=\text{const}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\ a\propto t^{1/3}\ }\quad(w=1)$$

So "the universe that always maximises its packing efficiency" is stiff (kination) — not ours. Here is the previous series' table of resources again.

①
Hold the bit count (horizon entropy) fixed\(a\propto e^{Ht}\) ── de Sitter
②
Hold the address space (comoving Hubble radius) fixed\(a\propto t\) ── \(c\cdot t=\)const
③
Hold the memory occupancy fixed\(a\propto t^{1/3}\) ── stiff (today's saturation condition)

Which resource you fix splits the answer three ways — and the observed universe is none of them (it changes over, radiation → matter → \(\Lambda\)). The very idea of "fixing one resource" is not enough to determine the shape of the universe — that is this episode's answer to Extra 2's question.

Extra 2's "what should be constrained is the light-sheet information" was right as a form — Bousso's bound is conformally invariant, gauge independent, and thus has the shape of something that can be judged in Episode 3's sense. But applied, the only place it bites is the Planck era.

Not a failure but a range determination Episode 16 measured the limit of the tool: "a conformal transformation touches only size". Today we measured the limit of the holographic bound — it says almost nothing about classical cosmology, only about the quantum gravity regime. The conclusion is that "the universe is a computer with finite resources" has meaning only at the Planck scale. Part V goes exactly there.
The honest line — what this episode assumes

① \(s\le3H/4\ell_P^2\) is Bousso's covariant entropy bound applied to one particular surface, the apparent horizon. The bound is a claim about arbitrary surfaces, so other surfaces could give stronger conditions — what is shown here is "at least on this surface the constraint is weak" (same caveat as §8 of Extra 3 in the previous series).

② Comoving conservation of entropy is assumed. With entropy production (reheating) the past \(s\) would have been smaller and the margin wider still (strengthening the conclusion).

③ "\(f\approx1\) at the Planck era" is a one-loop estimate. The coefficient moves with the choice of \(g_*\) (Extra 3 estimated \(T\le2.84M_{\rm Pl}/\sqrt{g_*}\) for standard cosmology). §02 claims that \(f\) comes out \(O(1)\) from dimensional analysis, not that the coefficient is exactly 1.

④ The \(f=1\) times in §03 assume a single \(p\) at all epochs. The 11 s for \(c\cdot t=\)const is the same order as Extra 3's 5 s, the difference coming from the value taken for today's \(f\). The real universe changes expansion laws, so tracing back with one \(p\) is a coarse approximation.

⑤ "No prediction" is a conclusion about this form of constraint, not a claim that the holographic principle is powerless — there are many contexts (AdS/CFT among them) where holography is a powerful computational tool. The point here is the single one that it is weak as a constraint determining a cosmological \(a(t)\).

Exercises (solvable with this episode's formulas alone)

  1. Derive the time dependence of \(f=s/(3H/4\ell_P^2)\) from \(a\propto t^p\).
    Show the answer
    \(s\propto a^{-3}\propto t^{-3p}\) and \(H\propto1/t\), so \(f\propto t^{-3p}/t^{-1}=t^{1-3p}\). Only at \(p=1/3\) does the exponent vanish.
  2. Why is \(f\approx1\) at the Planck era, and is it surprising?
    Show the answer
    With \(T\sim M_{\rm Pl}\), \(s\sim1/\ell_P^3\) and \(H\sim c/\ell_P\), substitution gives \(f\sim1\). It comes out of dimensional analysis alone, so in Episode 19's classification it is an identity — 0 bits of surprise. A check, not a discovery.
  3. Which expansion law saturates the bound identically?
    Show the answer
    The one making \(f\propto t^{1-3p}\) constant: \(p=1/3\), i.e. \(a\propto t^{1/3}\) (\(w=1\), stiff/kination). "The universe that always maximises packing efficiency" — but not ours.
  4. Imposed as a constraint, what does this bound exclude?
    Show the answer
    Only expansion laws reaching \(f>1\) before the Planck era. With 33 orders of margin today, essentially all standard ones pass — almost zero information as a constraint. Though \(c\cdot t=\)const applied at all epochs does violate it at \(t\sim10\) s.
  5. (Harder) Was Extra 2's "constrain the light-sheet information" wrong?
    Show the answer

Summary — the only place it bites was the Planck era

We went through the door the previous series left open — does constraining light-sheet information yield a prediction? Using Extra 3's \(s\le3H/4\ell_P^2\) and following the occupancy \(f\), it saturates exactly at the Planck era and the margin opens by 33 orders (16 at electroweak, 22 at nucleosynthesis, 33 today).

"Saturating exactly at the Planck era" looks meaningful, but measured with Episode 19's tool it was an identity — \(T\sim M_{\rm Pl}\) gives \(s\sim1/\ell_P^3\) and \(H\sim c/\ell_P\), so \(f\sim1\) from dimensional analysis alone. Zero bits of surprise. The third such case after Dirac's large numbers (Episode 7) and the Landauer limit (Episode 10).

And the heart — it is far too weak as a constraint. With 33 orders of margin, all it excludes are laws that violate before the Planck era, and standard ones all pass. Constraining light-sheet information yields no prediction. Saturating identically gives \(a\propto t^{1/3}\) (stiff), which is not our universe — fix the bit count and you get de Sitter, fix the address space and you get \(a\propto t\), fix the occupancy and you get \(a\propto t^{1/3}\). Which resource you fix splits the answer three ways, and the observed universe is none of them.

So the door is half closed. Extra 2's "constrain the light-sheet information" was right as a form — Bousso's bound is conformally invariant and gauge independent — but it bites only at the Planck era. Not a failure but a determination of range: as Episode 16 measured the limit of the conformal transformation, today measured the limit of the holographic bound. "The universe is a computer with finite resources" has meaning only in the quantum gravity regime. Part V goes there.
