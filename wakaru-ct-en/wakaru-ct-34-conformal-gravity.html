What you need: Episode 11's weight counting, Episode 19's practice, Episode 5's scales\(S\to\Omega^{D-4}S\) — invariant only in four dimensions

Every theory so far has used the conformal transformation as a rewriting bolted on afterwards. Mannheim does not — he puts conformal symmetry itself at the foundation of gravity. Replace the Einstein–Hilbert action with the square of the Weyl tensor and the whole theory is conformally invariant from the start. Then the cosmological constant problem disappears structurally, and rotation curves come out without dark matter. At a price.

06The price — ghosts

A fourth-order propagator $$\frac{1}{k^2(k^2+M^2)}=\frac{1}{M^2}\left[\frac{1}{k^2}-\frac{1}{k^2+M^2}\right]$$

the second term has a negative sign — a negative-norm state, a ghost

An interesting trade is happening

Episode 9 of the previous series: in Einstein gravity the conformal factor is a ghost.
In conformal gravity the conformal factor is pure gauge, so that problem disappears.
And the fourth-order derivatives bring in a new massive spin-2 ghost.
── The ghost was not removed but swapped for another.

Mannheim and Bender object — "the Hamiltonian is not Hermitian but PT-symmetric, and in a suitable inner product the eigenvalues are real and no negative-norm states appear". A serious published argument, but there is no consensus. This document does not adjudicate.

The honest line — what this episode assumes

① Conformal gravity is a minority theory, from a body of work since Mannheim & Kazanas (1989), and is not widely accepted. This document neither supports nor refutes it and measures only what this series' tools can measure.

② The ghost problem is unresolved. That fourth-order theories carry negative-norm states is a standard result, but Mannheim & Bender's PT-symmetry objection is also a peer-reviewed claim — there is no consensus, and this document does not adjudicate.

③ "The cosmological constant problem disappears structurally" applies before the symmetry breaks. Mass generation requires breaking it, and whether \(\Lambda\) returns afterwards is the point at issue — read "408 bits bought back for free" as conditional on that being resolved.

④ \(\gamma_*=5.42\times10^{-41}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}\) and \(\gamma_0=3.06\times10^{-28}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}\) are values Mannheim and collaborators obtained by fitting rotation curves. The crossover calculation is this document's, dropping the \(\kappa r^2\) and \(3\beta\gamma\) terms — an order-of-magnitude argument.

⑤ §08's ledger uses Episode 29's framework and has the same limitations. The parameter counts are crude and \(\Lambda\)CDM's priors are not accounted for — 1977 bits is an upper-side estimate.

⑥ There is no established result for conformal gravity's CMB predictions. That is the sense in which it is listed under "cannot buy" — not that its predictions have been shown to disagree with observation.

    Show the answer

  2. What is the difference, for this series, between \(G\) and \(\alpha_g\)?
    Show the answer
    \(G\) is dimensionful — bookkeeping — so moving it changes nothing (Episode 7). \(\alpha_g\) is dimensionless, weight 0, in the physics column of Episode 16's map — no escape, and measurement fixes it.
  3. Why can the cosmological constant term not be written, and what does that mean?
    Show the answer
    \(\int\sqrt{-g}\,\Lambda\,d^4x\) picks up \(\Omega^4\), so it is not conformally invariant and the symmetry forbids it. The 408-bit tuning counted in Episodes 12 and 32 disappears structurally, without adding parameters (though what happens after symmetry breaking is at issue).
  4. Find the crossover radius for a \(10^{11}M_\odot\) galaxy.
    Show the answer

  5. (Harder) What does Episode 3's surgery cut in conformal gravity?
    Show the answer
    Nothing. In Episodes 27–33 the conformal transformation was "another way of writing" (A); in conformal gravity it is a gauge symmetry, part of the theory's definition. The gravitational version of Episode 16's caveat ④ — which is why the price appears elsewhere, as ghosts.

Summary — the ghost was not removed but swapped

Mannheim places conformal symmetry not as an afterthought rewriting but as the foundation of gravity. With the Weyl-squared action, \(S\to\Omega^{D-4}S\), so it is conformally invariant only at \(D=4\) — exactly the structure of Episode 11's Maxwell action.

Three things follow. ① The gravitational coupling \(\alpha_g\) is dimensionless — unlike \(G\) it is not bookkeeping but sits in the physics column, fixed by measurement. ② The cosmological constant term picks up \(\Omega^4\), so the symmetry forbids it and Episode 32's 408-bit tuning disappears structurally, without adding parameters (contested after symmetry breaking). ③ The vacuum solution has a linear term \(\gamma r\) whose crossover for a \(10^{11}M_\odot\) galaxy is 44 kpc — exactly where rotation curves go flat.

But \(\gamma_0\) is on the cosmological scale too (\(\gamma_0\simeq1/(25R_H)\), surprise 5.4 bits) — essentially the stratum of MOND in Episode 29 (5.9 bits), the same shape of coincidence. And the ledger puts it in MOND's stratum too (about 1977 bits ahead of the halo model on rotation curves; the CMB and clusters unbought).

The price was ghosts. Fourth-order derivatives split the propagator into \(1/k^2-1/(k^2+M^2)\), the second with a negative sign. The trade is the interesting part — Episode 9 of the previous series saw the conformal factor as a ghost in Einstein gravity; in conformal gravity it becomes gauge and disappears, and a massive spin-2 ghost arrives instead. The ghost was not removed but swapped for another. As for the surgery — the conformal transformation is a gauge symmetry here, so there is nothing to cut. The one theory in Part IV that never made it onto the operating table.
