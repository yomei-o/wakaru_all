The honest line — for the series as a whole

(1) This series does not support \(c\cdot t=\)const. Episode 3 concluded that it is a notation and not a model, and the previous series' verdict (extrapolated at face value to the early universe it contradicts nucleosynthesis) has never moved — what 50 episodes examined was "brevity", not "correctness". The academic standard is the \(\Lambda\)CDM model including inflation, together with unmodified general relativity.

(2) Nearly all the physics presented here is established, standard material. Conformal transformations, the renormalisation group, black hole thermodynamics, quantum anomalies, the standard model of cosmology — none of it is a new claim. By contrast, "good theories have already had the surgery" (Ep. 36), "the place where the tool breaks moves" (Ep. 38), "it touches exactly half" (Ep. 45) and "there are four kinds of door" (Ep. 49) are readings this series found by laying things side by side, not textbook claims.

(3) Every count here is arbitrary in how it counts. "Nine theories", "eight places", "sixteen doors", "six tools" — all of them move with how you divide things up (Ep. 46 caveat 3, Ep. 49 caveat 1). The substance is the structure, not the count.

(4) The bit only shows that things can be written in one currency. Surprise, headroom, shortfall, gain, description length — these cannot be added together or ranked (Ep. 36 caveat 5, Ep. 45 caveat 3).

(5) There was something we could not measure. As Episode 48 said, sensory beauty lies outside this series' tools — not measurable is not the same as not real. And "do not use in a verdict what you cannot measure" has been the practice since Episode 3.

Summary: only one thing moves

Fifty episodes, six parts. That \(c\cdot t=\)const is a notation and not a model (Part I); that wherever you put it only size moves (Part II); that measured as information it was one number restated in eight languages (Part III); that applied to other theories the good ones had already had the surgery (Part IV); that the tool touches exactly half the world with the arrow of time in the other half (Part V); and that examining the procedure itself, there were only four kinds of door (Part VI).

Four times the compression worked — 24 numbers into 12 inputs, three \(10^{122}\)s into one, Penrose's \(10^{10^{123}}\) into that same one, and 12 ways of saying \(a\propto t\) into 3 inputs. All four times, numbers that looked separate were one. And every time, 0 bits — nothing remarkable was happening; the same thing was being said in different words.

Finally we applied that compression to the series itself. There were six tools, but Episodes 16 and 33 apply Episode 3 and Episodes 5, 19 and 24 all "measure in bits" — only two are independent. (i) Separating the dimensionful from the dimensionless, and (ii) measuring everything in bits. And one step further: (ii) was the tool for checking (i). What 50 episodes did was one idea, and a way of checking it.

The verdict never moved. \(c\cdot t=\)const is a notation and not new physics; extrapolated to the early universe it contradicts nucleosynthesis; \(q=0\) misses by 13\(\sigma\) and \(w=-1/3\) by 23\(\sigma\). What moved was not the verdict but the understanding of why that verdict can be reached.

Three lines are enough to take away — dimensionful is bookkeeping and dimensionless is physics; name what you compare to or it is not yet a sentence; surprise can be measured in bits. Those three redo all 50 episodes' worth of judging.

And the title. "Only one thing moves" — wherever a conformal transformation is applied only size moves, and what drove 50 episodes was one idea only. The two say the same thing. Separating what moves from what does not: that was the whole of this series.
