The heart of §02 ── one rung up

In electromagnetism the dipole survives (charge is conserved, but the "centre of charge" is not).
In gravity the motion of the centre of mass is conserved, so even the dipole dies.

Set the radiation formulas side by side ──

Electromagnetic (Episode 12): \(P=\dfrac{1}{6\pi\varepsilon_0c^3}\bigl\langle(\ddot{\mathbf{p}})^2\bigr\rangle\)　★ second derivative of a dipole, squared
Gravitational: \(P=\dfrac{G}{5c^5}\bigl\langle(\dddot{Q})^2\bigr\rangle\)　★ third derivative of a quadrupole, squared

★★ One more order in space buys one more order in time.

The heart of §03 ── the paradox of gravitational waves

For an instant GW150914 shone as brightly as every star in the universe. And that is still only a thousandth of \(c^5/G\).

★ Earth received a flux comparable to moonlight, and the arm moved by a thousandth of a proton.

The energy flowing is large; the coupling to matter is far too weak.

§04 ── a different chirp from Episode 13's

Exactly \(-3/8\), not the \(-1\) of Episode 13's hyperbolic chirp ── so it is not log-periodic.

05The speed of gravitational waves ── is there dispersion?

GW170817 ── gravitational waves and gamma rays arrived together $$D=1.23\times10^{24}\ \text{m}\ (40\ \text{Mpc}),\qquad \Delta t=1.7\ \text{s}$$ $$\frac{|\Delta v|}{c}<\frac{\Delta t}{D/c}=4.1\times10^{-16}$$

§05 ── translated into Episode 6's language

Gravitational waves and light travel at the same speed to \(10^{-15}\). That swept away many models in which gravity is faster or slower than light.

And it bounds the graviton mass ── as a bound on the cutoff frequency in \(\omega^2=c^2k^2+(m_gc^2/\hbar)^2\).

Exercises

  1. Why is the gravitational-wave frequency twice the orbital frequency?
    Show answer
    Because it is a quadrupole. The quadrupole moment \(\sum m\,r_ir_j\) returns to its original configuration after half an orbit (the two stars have swapped, and if their masses are equal they are indistinguishable).
    So the quadrupole repeats at half the orbital period, doubling the frequency.
    ★ In general \(2^s\)-pole radiation runs at \(s\) times the orbital frequency ── spin sets the multiplier.
  2. Explain "moonlight-level energy arrived and nothing happened" in Episode 6's language.
    Show answer
    Because the coupling is weak. A large flux does nothing if matter cannot absorb it.
    Light couples to charge with strength \(\alpha=1/137\). Gravity couples to mass with strength \(Gm^2/(\hbar c)\) ── for two protons, \(5.9\times10^{-39}\).
    ★ Thirty-six orders of magnitude. So the same flux passes almost entirely through.
    ★ And "not absorbed" is also a virtue ── nothing attenuates it, so the moment of a black-hole merger is visible from anywhere in the universe.
  3. What does it mean that the chirp exponent is \(-3/8\) here and \(-1\) in Episode 13?
    Show answer
    Whether there is a special moment.
    Episodes 13 and 20 gave \(1/t\) in a scale-invariant world where the origin of time carries no meaning (\(t\to\mu t\) leaves the form unchanged).
    A binary merger has a special moment \(t_c\), and \(\tau=t_c-t\) is meaningful. That breaks scale invariance and shifts the exponent away from \(-1\).
    ★ The \(-3/8\) comes from the quadrupole formula's \(f^{11/3}\) ── an instance of Episode 14's "dimensions and dynamics set the exponent".

Summary　Radiation one order higher

In gravitational waves, what waves is spacetime itself. The measured quantity \(h=\Delta L/L\) is dimensionless from the start ── Episode 7's conclusion has become a definition. GW150914's \(h=10^{-21}\) means a 4 km arm moved by a thousandth of a proton.

The monopole dies by mass conservation and the dipole by momentum conservation, so radiation starts at the quadrupole. Side by side ── electromagnetic is the second derivative of a dipole squared; gravitational is the third derivative of a quadrupole squared. ★ One order higher.

The reference power is \(c^5/G=3.6\times10^{52}\) W (built from \(G\) and \(c\) alone). GW150914's peak is a thousandth of it ── for an instant, as bright as every star in the universe. The flux at Earth was 3 mW/m², the order of moonlight, and the arm moved a thousandth of a proton. Large energy, far too weak a coupling.

The chirp goes as \(f\propto\tau^{-3/8}\) (verified numerically). It differs from Episode 13's \(1/t\) because there is a special moment: the merger. GW150914 takes 0.19 s from 35 Hz; the neutron stars take 37 s.

GW170817 gives \(|\Delta v|/c<4\times10^{-16}\). The polarisations sit at 45° because spin 2 means a rank-2 tensor ── spin, multipole order and polarisation angle are three names for one thing. And LIGO's sensitivity is already near the standard quantum limit, which is where Episode 22's squeezed light goes to work.

★ If electromagnetic radiation is the second derivative of a dipole, gravitational radiation is the third derivative of a quadrupole.
And what we measure is a ratio of lengths ── "only dimensionless is physics" is here a definition.
