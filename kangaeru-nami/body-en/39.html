01The same quadratic, twice

Episode 13: \(u''+\lambda u/r^2=0\) — the critical \(1/r^2\) potential $$u\sim r^s,\qquad s(s-1)+\lambda=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad s=\frac12\pm\sqrt{\frac14-\lambda}$$
A scalar in AdS (Poincaré coordinates, \(z\) radial) $$\varphi''-\frac{d-1}{z}\varphi'-\frac{m^2L^2}{z^2}\varphi=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \Delta^2-d\,\Delta-m^2L^2=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \Delta=\frac d2\pm\sqrt{\frac{d^2}4+m^2L^2}$$

The point of §01

Set \(d\to1\) and \(m^2L^2\to-\lambda\) — ★ they are literally the same equation.

Both have a \(1/(\text{distance})^2\) potential, both produce two powers, and both have their critical point where the discriminant changes sign.

02Solve it, and measure the boundary two-point function

Words are cheap, so we solve the wave equation numerically. With boundary momentum \(k\) included:

Setting \(t=\ln z\) makes the radial direction a clean first-order system $$\frac{d\varphi}{dt}=\psi,\qquad \frac{d\psi}{dt}=d\,\psi+\bigl(k^2e^{2t}+m^2L^2\bigr)\varphi$$

Start deep inside (large \(z\)) with the decaying solution, integrate out to the boundary (\(z\to0\)), and decompose \(\varphi=A z^{\Delta_-}+B z^{\Delta_+}\).
For \(d=4,\ m^2L^2=-3.75\) (\(\nu=1/2\)) — the exact answer is \(B/A=\frac{\Gamma(-\nu)}{\Gamma(\nu)}(k/2)^{2\nu}=-k\).

Exercises

  1. §05 says the wavelet plane and AdS share a metric. Is that a coincidence?
    Show answer
    No. Both come from the same symmetry — translation plus dilation (the affine group).
    ★ The hyperbolic upper half plane is the affine group's homogeneous space, and \(\mathrm{AdS}_2\) is another name for it. Any scale-invariant theory automatically carries this geometry.
    ★★ But "same metric" is not "same physics" — the wavelet plane has no dynamics (no Einstein equation). The stage can have the same shape while what happens on it is another matter. Per the discipline of Episodes 29–30, we stop at "the symmetry is the same."
  2. §03 found the order to be the square root itself. What does that add to Episode 14's "reasons the order leaves the integers"?
    Show answer
    Episode 14's three were ① superposition of many time constants (Ep. 2), ② dimension (Ep. 30), ③ continuous variation at a critical point (Ep. 13).
    ★ The fourth is the bulk mass — \(\alpha=2\sqrt{d^2/4+m^2L^2}\). A mass, a continuously adjustable quantity, moves the order continuously.
    ★★ What is striking is that this one contains ② and ③ at once: \(d\) appears in it, so it is about dimension, and the thing under the root changes sign, so it is about criticality. Holography packs the reasons for a non-integer order into a single formula.
  3. §06's entropy depends on \(\varepsilon\). Is that a flaw in the theory?
    Show answer
    Not a flaw — it is inevitable. Until you say how finely you look, the number of degrees of freedom is not defined — the same point as Episode 22's window.
    ★ What is physical is the difference: \(S(\ell_2)-S(\ell_1)=\frac c3\ln(\ell_2/\ell_1)\) contains no \(\varepsilon\). "Only dimensionless quantities are physics" (Episodes 7, 15) is at work again.
    ★★ And separating the \(\varepsilon\)-dependent part from the rest is renormalization — which, as §07 showed, is motion in the radial direction.

Summary — the extra dimension is the log-scale axis

The AdS scalar's relation \(\Delta=\frac d2\pm\sqrt{\frac{d^2}4+m^2L^2}\) is the same quadratic as Episode 13's \(s=\frac12\pm\sqrt{\frac14-\lambda}\) (\(d\to1,\ m^2L^2\to-\lambda\)). Hence the BF bound \(m^2L^2=-d^2/4\) is Episode 13's critical \(\lambda=1/4\).

We solved the wave equation numerically and measured the boundary two-point function — the slope of \(\ln|B/A|\) against \(\ln k\) is 1.000000 (prediction \(2\nu=1\)), matching the exact \(B/A=-k\) to five digits. Just integrating a wave from inside out produces a correlation function's power.

★★★ The core — the bulk mass sets the boundary's order.
\(\alpha=2\sqrt{d^2/4+m^2L^2}\)
The BF bound is where it hits zero (a logarithm); below it the order is imaginary.

Indeed, past the bound at \(m^2L^2=-4.25\) the zeros are equally spaced in \(\ln z\) (gap 6.283185, matching \(\pi/|\nu|\) to \(1.7\times10^{-9}\), ratio in \(z\) 535.49) — the same log-periodicity as Episode 13's Efimov states.

★★ And — the wavelet plane's metric \((db^2+da^2)/a^2\) is literally the AdS metric. Radial \(z\) = scale \(a\) = \(1/\omega\). Because a scale transformation is an isometry, the log-scale axis acquires a distance and can act as a dimension. Episode 38's "17 decades from electroweak to Planck" becomes a proper distance of 39.14 \(L\) — the hierarchy problem turns into a length.

Entanglement entropy is a geodesic length \(2\ln(\ell/\varepsilon)\) (matching to \(10^{-11}\)) — a logarithm = order zero, the same place as Episode 2's 1/f. The degrees of freedom count as \((L/\varepsilon)^{d-1}\) — Episode 6's holographic bound. And the renormalization group, in the extra dimension, is just translation (Episode 5).

★ Honestly: the duality itself was assumed, not tested. AdS is not our universe either. ★ Once again this is a translation, not a new prediction.
