02Shannon's formula was "bandwidth × time"

Integrate information theory's basic formula $$C=B\log_2\Bigl(1+\frac{S}{N}\Bigr)\ \text{[bit/s]}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad I=C\,T=\underbrace{B\,T}_{\text{band}\times\text{time}}\cdot\log_2\Bigl(1+\frac{S}{N}\Bigr)$$

The heart of §02

information ＝ (bandwidth × time) × (bits per sample)

★ Episode 9's bandwidth theorem \(\Delta t\,\Delta\omega\ge1/2\) drops straight out.
By Nyquist's sampling theorem, a signal of bandwidth \(B\) and duration \(T\) has \(2BT\) degrees of freedom ── that is the number of "modes".

★ "To send one bit you need time × bandwidth."

Exercises

  1. Is "raise S/N without limit and any narrow band carries infinite information" correct?
    Show answer
    Correct on paper, wrong physically.
    \(C=B\log_2(1+S/N)\) diverges as \(S/N\to\infty\). But raising \(S\) costs energy, and raising energy runs into §04's bound (\(\nu\le2E/\pi\hbar\)).
    ★ Moreover \(N\) has a floor ── quantum noise. Each mode carries at least \(\hbar\omega/2\) of zero-point fluctuation, which cannot be removed (Episode 9's bandwidth theorem again).
    ★ So "you must widen the band" is physically right as well.
  2. What does "information lives on the imaginary axis" mean in practice?
    Show answer
    The same as Episode 19 ── you need not wait a full period to measure it.
    An entropy production rate is fixed by a short observation (being monotone, you just look at the slope).
    ★ Conversely it does not come back. Real-axis quantities conserve phase and can be rewound; imaginary-axis quantities have thrown information away and cannot.
    ★ "Erasing a computation costs energy" (Landauer) is exactly this ── erasure is an imaginary-axis operation, so it must be paid for in heat.
  3. If \(c\cdot t=\)const and "constant information" are the same thing, what is cosmic expansion in the language of information?
    Show answer
    The ruler is gaining tick marks.
    Since \(I\propto(ct)^2\), a growing horizon means more distinguishable states.
    ★ The \(c\cdot t=\)const system absorbs that growth into the units ── so nothing grows there, and time becomes logarithmic instead (Episode 20).
    ★ Verdict: a correspondence, not a claim about cosmology. Note also that the holographic bound is an upper bound, not the number of bits actually in use.

Summary　The limits on information were all the bandwidth theorem

Integrate a bit rate [bit/s] and you get information [bit] ── the same shape as speed and distance. And the constant of integration is the initial information, undeterminable in principle (Episode 3).

Shannon's formula is \(I=BT\log_2(1+S/N)\), that is "bandwidth × time" ── Episode 9's bandwidth theorem itself. The telephone line's 30.9 kbps was the dial-up wall, and optical fibre is nine orders faster because its band is nine orders wider.

Landauer's limit is 0.0179 eV/bit at room temperature, with semiconductors still ten thousand times above it. The speed bound (\(5.4\times10^{50}\) /s·kg) and the capacity bound (\(9\times10^{44}\) bit for a human) are all restatements of one theorem: energy sets bandwidth.

The universe's information goes as \(I\propto(ct)^2\), so it can be differentiated ── \(1.4\times10^{105}\) bit/s today, with a constant information acceleration of \(3.1\times10^{87}\) bit/s². At the Planck time there were 4.5 bits.

★ And ── the \(c\cdot t=\)const coordinate system is also the one that holds the universe's information constant (Episode 20 called it logarithmic time).

★ Information lives on the imaginary axis, and so does not come back.
"The arrow of time" was the imaginary axis of the complex frequency plane.

On the numbers: the Shannon capacities are theoretical values for assumed bandwidths and S/N; real systems differ through coding, multiplexing and MIMO (optical fibre exceeds the computed value because wavelength- and polarisation-division multiplexing effectively widen the band). The switching energy \(\frac12CV^2\) varies by orders with process and design and is not a figure for any particular product. The Bekenstein and holographic bounds are upper bounds, not information actually in use ── a human does not hold \(9\times10^{44}\) bits. "Constant information acceleration" follows from writing the holographic bound as \(I=\pi(ct)^2/(l_P^2\ln2)\), and both the coefficient and the time dependence change with the choice of horizon (Hubble, particle, event) ── we took the most naive choice, \(ct\).
