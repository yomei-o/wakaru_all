The point of §04 — ★★★ the most productive failure

Episode 32: we predicted that once the symmetry broke, the field would settle in the trough. It did not. It keeps oscillating between \(\pm\sqrt2\,v\) indefinitely.
★★★ The conclusion that came out of the failure was larger than the prediction — "symmetry breaking requires dissipation." Nonlinearity does not stop the oscillation; dissipation does.
★ And that conclusion paid off directly in Episode 40 (time crystals) — you need both drive and dissipation, so an equilibrium time crystal is impossible.
★★ Not deleting the calculations we lost has been this series' policy since Episode 15.

05★ Where the series corrected itself

Episode 29 corrects Episode 15

Episode 15 used the single word "order" for two different quantities:
　・the order of differentiation (the \(\alpha\) of fractional calculus; \(1/f\) is order \(1/2\))
　・the order of an equation (how many time derivatives; the wave equation is second order)
★ When Episode 29 showed that anything above second order is forbidden by Ostrogradsky, the two came apart.
★★ Combined with Episodes 17–18 (passivity) — odd orders are killed by passivity, even orders by Ostrogradsky. The reason physics lives in a narrow rectangle came from two entirely separate theorems.

Exercises

  1. All 25 items passed in §03. Does that mean the series is right?
    Show answer
    No — it means there are no arithmetic errors. What was recomputed is numbers this series derived from formulas this series wrote; whether the formulas themselves are right is a separate question.
    ★ Episode 39's \(\alpha=2\nu\), for instance, assumes the AdS/CFT correspondence. If the correspondence fails, agreeing numbers mean nothing.
    ★★ A regression test only protects today's self from contradicting yesterday's. What protects against contradicting nature is §04's list of failed predictions. ★ That is exactly why Episode 15 ruled: having declared the hypotheses falsifiable, go looking for falsifications.
  2. §06 says no new formula came out. Then what were these 27 episodes doing?
    Show answer
    Re-shelving. Re-shelving does three things — ① you notice when the same thing shows up twice (Episode 39's \(\Delta\) and Episode 13's \(s\)); ② you notice what is missing (Episode 14's three lacked ④ and ⑤); ③ you learn which quantities are sensitive (Episode 42's 0.081 dB/oct).
    ★ ③ is the most practical — an indicator that "fits well" may merely be insensitive.
    ★★ But this is organizing, not discovering. ★ Not calling organizing a discovery is the thing this series guards most carefully.
  3. §07 says the axis was tested twice. Where would a third test come from?
    Show answer
    From somewhere the order cannot be defined at all. So far we have had ① a quantity that does not fit (integers) and ② not being one number (a distribution). What remains is ③ a field with no power law in the first place.
    ★ A finite system, a strongly non-equilibrium one, or a critical point where a logarithm replaces the power — Episode 39's BF bound and Episode 42's \(\zeta_3\) already brush against it (both are points where a power dies into a logarithm).
    ★★ Saying "a logarithm is order zero" puts it back on the axis, but that is only a convention for putting it there, and some cases will refuse. ★ Find one, and you will have found the axis's true edge.

Summary — the axis held, but it is no longer single

Twenty-seven episodes since Episode 15. Every headline number was recomputed independently and checked — 25 items, zero FAILs. Only Episode 37's Watson integral remains 0.4 % out, because the grid cannot resolve the singularity at the origin.

Episode 1's \(\text{dB/oct}=6\alpha\) and \(\text{phase}=90\alpha\) were still the axis 42 episodes later. Along the way aliases kept appearing — dimension \(d=2\alpha+3\), Hurst \(H=\alpha-\frac12\), AdS's \(\nu\), the structure-function \(\zeta_p\) — which is what made "turbulence is 5/6-order" sayable at all.

The reasons the order leaves the integers went from three to five — ④ the bulk mass (Ep. 39) and ⑤ the order not being a single number (Ep. 42). ④ folds ② and ③ into one formula; only ⑤ is qualitatively different.

★ Ten predictions failed, and all of them are still in the text. The most productive was Episode 32 — "the field settles in the trough" was wrong; it oscillates at \(\pm\sqrt2\,v\) forever. The conclusion drawn from that failure — "symmetry breaking requires dissipation" — was bigger than the prediction, and paid off directly in Episode 40.

★ One self-correction: Episode 15 conflated the order of differentiation with the order of an equation. Episode 29 separated them — odd orders are killed by passivity, even orders by Ostrogradsky.

★★★ And honestly — no formula unknown to humanity came out of this.
What came out is "another reading of the same formula" and "what to measure."

★ In these 27 episodes the axis was tested twice — Episode 41 produced a quantity that does not sit on it (integers: winding and Chern numbers), and Episode 42 produced a place where it is not single (a distribution of orders). Neither broke it, but both drew its outline.
