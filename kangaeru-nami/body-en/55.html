Exercises

  1. §03 (a) found 0.0812 good to two figures. Does Episode 42's conclusion change?
    Show answer
    The conclusion does not change; the way it is written does. Episode 42's claim was "intermittency barely moves the dB/oct, which is why the power spectrum cannot see it" — true at 0.04 and at 0.13 alike.
    ★ What changes is having written "0.081" to three figures — it should read "of order 0.1 dB/oct."
    ★★ And the comparison with Episode 47 ("both the universe and turbulence are of order 0.1 dB/oct") is more accurate in this phrasing — since neither is known better than one figure.
    ★ Saying "the same order" was right; setting "0.0812 against 0.1053" side by side overstated the precision.
  2. §03 (c) says the logarithm makes the answer insensitive to definitions. Does that contradict Episode 53?
    Show answer
    No — the same property, seen from both sides. A logarithm barely moves when its input moves a lot.
    ★ In Episode 53 that was bad news (\(10^6\) times the data buys 3× the range).
    ★★ Here it is good news (changing the band definition by \(10^{18}\) changes the answer by 2×).
    ★★★ "Hard to increase" and "hard to shake" are the same thing. So quantities spoken of in e-folds can be discussed without pinning down the definition — which is why "60 to 75" sufficed in Episodes 46 and 47.
  3. §04 found Koide's \(K\) indistinguishable from \(2/3\). So is the relation right?
    Show answer
    It cannot be called right, and it cannot be called wrong — that is the present state. Difference ÷ uncertainty = 0.909 means only that the discrepancy is within chance.
    ★ What matters is that this is a measurable question — reduce \(m_\tau\)'s uncertainty tenfold and the ratio becomes 9, settling it.
    ★★ Episode 15 ruled that having declared the hypotheses falsifiable, we go looking for falsifications. Koide's relation is exactly of that shape, and tau-mass measurements will decide it automatically.
    ★ Conversely, the reading this series offered in Episode 33 — "\(K=2/3\iff\) the AC rms equals the DC" — is independent of whether the relation is exact: it is a translation, not a claim.

Summary — we had been writing too many digits

Episodes 51–54 are folded into the handbook, taking it from 7 steps to 10 — ⑧ split the error into statistical and systematic, ⑨ buy range if it is short, ⑩ count the assumptions.

Sorting 16 of this series' numbers — 6 computed here, 3 from literature, 5 conditional on a model, 2 translations. The five conditional ones now carry sensitivities.

★★★ The core —
Episode 42's 0.0812 moves between 0.0446 and 0.1311 as \(p\) swings over 0.65–0.75.
Episode 47's \(-0.1053\) carries \(\pm0.0126\) from the observation.
Both are good to two significant figures.

★ This series has been writing "0.0812" and "\(-0.1053\)" to four figures — too many. The right phrasing is "of order 0.1 dB/oct." ★ But the conclusions of Episodes 42 and 47 (intermittency is hard to see; universe and turbulence are the same order) survive at this precision.

★ The logarithm's good side also appeared — Episode 46's "1.5 ms" changes by only a factor of two when the band's lower edge moves by \(10^{18}\). Episode 53's "hard to increase" and this "hard to shake" are one property seen twice.

★★ As a worked case, Koide's \(K\) — the difference from \(2/3\) is \(-6.16\times10^{-6}\) against an uncertainty of \(\pm6.78\times10^{-6}\) from \(m_\tau\). Difference ÷ uncertainty = 0.909: indistinguishable at present. ★ What matters is that this is a measurable question — tau-mass measurements will settle it.
