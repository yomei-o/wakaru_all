The point of §01

Up to the factor \(\sqrt2\), it is one line. In wave language — the Planck length is "the length at which a wave, made short enough, traps itself with its own energy."
★ It is the limit of Episode 6's "mass = cutoff frequency" — push the cutoff up by adding energy, and gravity makes a cutoff of its own.

From electroweak (246 GeV, \(8\times10^{-19}\) m) to Planck is 17 decades — that distance is Episode 15's hierarchy problem.

03★★ What many theories agree on: dimension runs

Candidate theories of quantum gravity differ enormously, but there is one thing they tend to agree on — at short distances the "dimension" drops from 4 to 2 (spectral dimension reduction).
Spectral dimension is dimension measured by diffusion (the quantity we used in Episode 37) — \(P(s)\propto s^{-d_s/2}\).

When the dispersion relation is \(\omega\propto k^z\) (\(z=1\) is an ordinary wave) $$d_s=1+\frac{\text{spatial dimension}}{z}$$

Rather than assert it, we measure it. We take the spatial part from \(P(s)=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}e^{-s(k^2)^z}\) and add the unmodified time direction.

The point of §04

★★★ At \(d=2\), gravity's coupling is exactly dimensionless.
★ So the expectation "\(d_s\to2\) in the ultraviolet" is — a restatement of the wish that "the coupling become dimensionless in the ultraviolet."

★ Put beside Episode 25's dimensional transmutation — QCD makes a dimensionful \(\Lambda\) out of a dimensionless \(\alpha_s\). Quantum gravity wants the reverse: to make a dimensionful \(G\) dimensionless. Both are demands of "only dimensionless quantities are physics" (Episodes 7 and 15).

Exercises

  1. People often say "no length shorter than the Planck length exists." Does §01 show that?
    Show answer
    No. All §01 showed is that "if you try to measure that length with a wave, the measuring wave itself makes a black hole."
    ★ "Cannot be measured" and "does not exist" are different — the same caution as Episode 5's "a period longer than the age of the universe is indistinguishable from a constant." Indistinguishability is not proof of absence.
    ★★ The question "is there any point writing into a theory something that cannot be measured?" does remain, and the answer depends on one's stance. What wave language can speak to is the limit of measurement.
  2. In §03, \(d_s=2\) coincided with Episode 30's critical point, but we held it as pending. What would settle it?
    Show answer
    Redefining both \(\alpha\)'s from the same quantity. Episode 30's \(\alpha\) is the order of the Green function's singularity; this episode's \(d_s\) is the exponent of a diffusion return probability.
    ★ To settle it you would have to actually build the Green function of the modified dispersion relation and measure its front exponent — then "is it the same \(\alpha\)?" becomes a number.
    ★★ This episode did not do that, so it stays pending. Having decided in Episodes 29–30 that "when you see the same number, check what you divided by," anything unchecked does not become a claim.
  3. §05 said the first-order effect is excluded. Does that mean "there is no quantum-gravity effect"?
    Show answer
    No — only a particular form was excluded. What was measured is the first-order dependence \(\Delta t\propto E/E_P\); at second order, \(\Delta t\propto(E/E_P)^2\), the effect is \(10^{19}\) times smaller and utterly out of reach.
    ★ And most theories predict second order to begin with (first order breaks Lorentz invariance so strongly that other experiments constrain it too).
    ★★ So this exclusion means "the easiest form to measure is gone" — the search just got harder. On Episode 15's scorecard it would read "× excluded (under the loosest assumption only)."

Summary — dimension running is order running

The Planck length comes out in one line — where a wavelength equals its own Schwarzschild radius (\(1.616\times10^{-35}\) m). It is the limit of Episode 6's "mass = cutoff frequency": push the cutoff up and gravity makes a cutoff of its own.

Gravity alone is non-renormalizable because its coupling carries the dimension \([E]^{-2}\). The effective coupling \((E/E_P)^2\) passes 1 at Planck — the opposite of Episode 34's Yang–Mills; in wave language, "a medium in which shorter waves interact more strongly."

★★★ And the core of this episode — the widely shared expectation that \(d_s\) runs 4 → 2 at short distance,
read through Episode 30's \(\alpha=(d-3)/2\), was
"order runs from \(+1/2\) to \(-1/2\)."

We checked it numerically — from \(P(s)=\int d^3k\,e^{-s(k^2)^z}\) the spatial part is exactly \(3/z\), and adding the time direction gives \(d_s=1+3/z\). \(z=1\to d_s=4\); \(z=3\to d_s=2\).

★ Why it stops at 2 also falls out — \([G]=[E]^{2-d}\), so at \(d=2\) gravity's coupling is exactly dimensionless. "\(d_s\to2\) in the UV" was a restatement of "let the coupling become dimensionless in the UV." Beside Episode 25's dimensional transmutation (QCD makes a dimension out of a dimensionless number) it runs the other way, and both are demands of "only dimensionless quantities are physics."

★ That \(d_s=2\) coincides with Episode 30's critical \(\lambda=1/4\) is an observation, not a claim — separately defined quantities, so we held it as pending (the discipline of Episodes 29–30).

Finally — translating makes clear what to measure. If \(z\ne1\), group velocity depends on frequency, and a 10 GeV photon from a GRB arrives 0.3 s apart. ★ And it has been measured: the first-order effect is already excluded (up to the Planck scale). ★ This episode only put an existing expectation into other words; it produced no new prediction.
