From sound to the physical constants

Thinking in Waves Differentiate an audio signal and it gets brighter; integrate it and it gets darker ── exactly \(\pm6.02\) dB per octave.
Physics does the same thing. Position → velocity → acceleration is an operation that keeps tilting a spectrum.
So are the physical constants all integrals of something? ── we followed that to the end.

Where the series starts

On the Fourier side, differentiation and integration are just multiplications.

$$\frac{d}{dt}\ \longrightarrow\ \times(i\omega),\qquad \int dt\ \longrightarrow\ \times\frac{1}{i\omega}$$

Amplitude by \(\omega\), phase by \(90°\). So differentiation boosts the highs (+6 dB/oct) and integration the lows (−6 dB/oct).
Repeating calculus in physics means tilting a signal's spectrum over and over.

House rules Every number is computed here, and we distinguish "measured", "a published value" and "our own hypothesis". Hypotheses are stated as hypotheses, and when they fail we say so.
In this series three conjectures were refuted and one was literally confirmed ── both are left on the page.
Part I
Building the tools
First, check whether the foundation is really exact. Then make the gap between differentiation and integration continuous.
Episode 1 out the start

Differentiation is treble, integration is bass ── first, let us measure it

We actually differentiated and integrated a 48 kHz synthetic signal. Differentiation multiplies the amplitude by \(\omega\) and rotates the phase by \(+90°\); integration by \(1/\omega\) and \(-90°\). Two octaves give \(\pm12.04\) dB ── exactly \(\pm6\) dB/oct.
Even the small discrepancy at high frequency is explained to six decimal places by the central difference's \(\mathrm{sinc}(\omega\Delta t)\). Approximation, not physics.
And physics does nothing but repeat this ── position → velocity → acceleration. \(F=ma\) is "the thing tilted by +12 dB/oct".

Calculus in physics is the operation of tilting a spectrum
Episode 2 out going continuous

Differentiate by one half ── and it turns out to be real

There is no reason to keep the exponent an integer. Moving \(\alpha\) continuously gives dB/oct \(=6\alpha\), phase \(=90\alpha\) degrees ── a clean interpolation.
And \(\alpha=-1/2\) (half-integration) is real ── the Warburg impedance \(Z\propto(i\omega)^{-1/2}\), measured daily in battery laboratories and visible as the 45° line on a Nyquist plot.
Fractional orders have memory (an integral kernel appears). [Hypothesis] Physics is nearly integer-order because nature is local. For noise, \(\beta=-2\alpha\) holds exactly, and 1/f is unreachable at integer orders.

Lower bound α=−1/2 (the diffusion boundary), upper bound α=2 (Ostrogradsky)
Part II
Constants as waves
The constant of integration lives at ω=0. So perhaps the physical constants live there too.
Episode 3 out the bridge

Is the constant of integration the physical constant?

Integration is \(\times1/(i\omega)\), so it diverges at \(\omega=0\) and cannot fix the DC component. That is what "+\(C\)" really is ── the \(C\) you met at school is the origin of a spectrum.
And with only a window \(T\), we cannot distinguish a wave of period \(>T\) from a constant (even the age of the universe bottoms out at \(2.3\times10^{-18}\) Hz).
Constants of integration come in two kinds ── invisible ones (the zero of potential, gauge) become symmetries; visible ones (the cosmological constant \(\Lambda\)) become new constants.

A physical constant = a component with period longer than the age of the universe
Episode 4 out experiment

Differentiating a constant ── the speed of light became undifferentiable in 1983

\(c\) is a defined value. \(dc/dt=0\) is a convention, not a measurement ── and the 2019 SI revision gave seven constants the same treatment. Only dimensionless quantities carry meaning.
Their derivatives have been measured ── \(|\dot\alpha/\alpha|<10^{-17}\)/yr. Over the whole history of the universe \(\alpha\) is constant to seven decimal places.
Translated into frequency bands, the lead changes hands at a period of 32 Gyr (optical clocks → the Oklo natural reactor). A tabletop clock fights cosmic history to a draw. But Fourier assumes stationarity, so a history that "jumped once" escapes ── the framework's first limit.

"Differentiating the constants" is already an experimental programme
Episode 5 out ★ a hit

The running coupling ── here the constant really was an integral

Differentiating in time gave zero. We had the wrong axis.
Computing the one-loop vacuum polarisation gives \(\Delta\alpha_{\text{lep}}(m_Z)=\mathbf{0.031421}\) (published 0.031498). Adding the hadronic part gives \(1/\alpha(m_Z)=128.940\) (published 128.95).
\(1/137.036\to1/128.940\), a 5.9 % change. And that change is \(\int\beta(\alpha)\,d\ln\mu\) ── "constants are integrals" is literally true here. Only the variable is \(\ln(\text{energy})\), not time.

Whether something is constant depends on which axis's spectrum you look at
Part III
Physics as waves
The constants are settled. Now the physical quantities themselves ── and then we score them.
Episode 6 out ★ a hit

Mass is a cutoff frequency ── the vacuum was a waveguide

The Klein–Gordon dispersion relation \(\omega^2=c^2k^2+(mc^2/\hbar)^2\) is word for word the waveguide formula, with \(\omega_c=mc^2/\hbar\) the cutoff.
Below cutoff the wavenumber goes imaginary and the field decays over \(\hbar/mc\) ── the Yukawa potential, and the true identity of a force's range. 1.41 fm for the pion (the size of a nucleus); \(2.5\times10^{-3}\) fm for the W (why the weak force is weak).
\(E=mc^2\) is "the energy of the cutoff", \(E^2=(pc)^2+(mc^2)^2\) is the dispersion relation itself. Velocity is \(d\omega/dk\) ── the derivative of a spectrum.

Mass is not an integral but a filter characteristic
Episode 7 out scoring

Scoring two conjectures ── the neat 1/2 turned out to be an identity

Mass = the integral of gravity: Sciama's relation gives \(GM/(Rc^2)=0.500000\) ── exactly 1/2. But substituting makes \(G\), \(c\) and \(H\) all vanish; in a critical-density universe it is an identity. The testable version (Brans–Dicke) is excluded by Cassini at the \(10^{-5}\) level.
Light speed = the integral of information: the holographic bound is computable (\(3.3\times10^{122}\) bits, \(2.2\times10^{121}\) operations). But \(l_P/t_P=c\) is a definition, not a discovery.
Where the idea pays off is not the constants but the relations between them.

The hits and misses split along dimension
Part IV
Constraints imposed on waves
Being a wave is not freedom but constraint. Causality, uncertainty and the arrow of time all come from the same place.
Episode 8 out constraint 1

Causality ── a filter does not know the future

From the single condition "no output before the input", the real and imaginary parts completely determine each other ── the Kramers–Kronig relations.
Verified numerically on a Lorentz oscillator to 13 digits (subtracting the singularity before taking the principal value).
Hence "no dispersion without absorption" ── even a transparent medium has a refractive index different from 1 only because it absorbs somewhere far away. And the sum rule \(\int\omega\varepsilon''d\omega=\pi\omega_p^2/2\) merely counts electrons.

Causality = analyticity. Real and imaginary parts are not independent
Episode 9 out constraint 2

The uncertainty principle is the bandwidth theorem times \(\hbar\)

In the world of sound it is obvious ── a short sound has no definite pitch. That is \(\Delta t\,\Delta\omega\ge1/2\) (the Gabor limit); numerically, a Gaussian gives exactly 0.5000.
Multiply both sides by \(\hbar\) and you get \(\Delta E\,\Delta t\ge\hbar/2\) ── the uncertainty principle, unchanged. Not a quantum mystery but a Fourier theorem.
There is measurement too ── \(^{57}\)Fe with its 141 ns lifetime has a natural width \(\Gamma/E=3.24\times10^{-13}\), and Mössbauer spectroscopy used that sharpness to measure a gravitational redshift over 22.5 m.

Uncertainty is nothing but a consequence of being a wave
Episode 10 out constraint 3

The arrow of time lives in the parity of the derivative order

Under \(t\to-t\), \(D^n\to(-1)^nD^n\) ── even orders survive, odd orders flip. So \(F=ma\) (order 2) is reversible and diffusion (order 1) is not.
Fractional orders interpolate continuously: dissipation \(\propto\sin(\alpha\pi/2)\), confirmed to \(10^{-14}\). \(\alpha=0\) is perfectly elastic, \(\alpha=1\) perfectly viscous, and real materials lie in between.
★ Fused silica is \(\alpha=6.4\times10^{-6}\), asphalt \(\alpha=0.5000\) ── the "strength" of the arrow of time is a measurable material property.

Reversible or not is set by the parity of the order
Part V
Physical quantities as waves
The tools are ready. Now apply them one after another ── heat, electromagnetic waves, and the colour of the sky.
Episode 11 out application 1

Heat does not travel as a wave ── first order in time, second in space

The wave equation is second order in both; the diffusion equation is first in time and second in space. That mismatch gives the purely imaginary dispersion relation \(\omega=-iDk^2\) ── decay instead of propagation.
The penetration depth is \(\delta=\sqrt{2D/\omega}\) ── in soil, 0.117 m daily and 2.241 m seasonally. Seven metres down the seasons vanish.
And the electromagnetic skin effect is exactly the same formula (9.23 mm at 50 Hz in copper, 2.06 μm at 1 GHz). Heat and current share one equation.

The balance of orders separates waves from diffusion
Episode 12 out application 2

Radiation comes from the second derivative ── which is why the sky is blue

Expand the field of a charge in distance and only the \(1/r\) term is proportional to acceleration and carries energy. "Radiation requires acceleration" was a question of order.
From the Larmor formula, a classical hydrogen atom collapses in 16 picoseconds ── one of the reasons quantum mechanics was needed.
And squaring the second derivative gives \(\omega^4\) ── Rayleigh scattering; blue scatters 4.4 times more than red. Go to third order (radiation reaction) and pre-acceleration appears, breaking causality.

The sky is blue because we squared a second derivative
Part VI
Extending the order into the complex plane
Episode 2 made the order real. Making it imaginary produced a completely different physics.
Episode 13 out ★ new ground

Imaginary-order differentiation ── the tool of the world of scale

\((i\omega)^{i\beta}=e^{-\beta\pi/2}e^{i\beta\ln\omega}\) ── the amplitude is a constant independent of \(\omega\); only the phase rotates as \(\ln\omega\): an all-pass filter (confirmed to 12 digits with white noise). It stretches time without touching the timbre.
The impulse response has the closed form \(t\,h(t)=A(\beta)\sin(\beta\ln t-\arg\Gamma(1+i\beta))\) ── a sinusoid on a logarithmic scale. But the amplitude is crushed by \(e^{-\beta\pi}\), already \(10^{-6}\) at \(\beta=3\). Which is why log-periodicity is hard to find.
★ If real orders are the tool of time translation (Fourier), imaginary orders are the tool of scale transformation (Mellin). In physics the imaginary part turns on only when an attractive \(1/r^2\) passes \(\lambda=1/4\) ── measured as the Efimov ladder with ratio 22.7.

The order is a plane, not a line, and physics lives in one narrow corner
Episode 14 out ★ a finding

The ladder of spectral exponents ── only three ways to leave the integers

Line up every slope in this series and almost all are integers or half-integers; the exceptions are the turbulent \(-5/3\), \(-11/5\) and \(-7/3\).
Tracking their denominators gives ── ★ the determinant of the dimensional matrix. \(|\det M|=3\) for Kolmogorov, 5 for Bolgiano–Obukhov, and 1 for Phillips (ocean waves), hence the integer \(-5\). Whether you get an integer was decided by a determinant.
★ And turbulence's \(-5/3\) is not evidence of fractional order ── Navier–Stokes stays integer-order and the fraction appears after taking statistics. Quite different in provenance from Warburg's \(\alpha=-1/2\).

A fractional order, a division of dimensions, or a complex order ── only three
Part VII
Interim summing-up
Fifteen episodes in, count what was right and what was wrong ── and notice that the protagonist had changed.
Episode 15 out interim summary

What physics can be written in wave language, and what cannot

Twelve claims stood, five fell. There were two dividing lines ── ① hits are dimensionless, misses dimensionful; ② the order of a law and the exponent of a statistic are different things.
★ And the protagonist had changed along the way. Causality, uncertainty, the arrow of time, why heat does not travel, why the sky is blue, turbulence's exponent ── all of it was about the order.
Physics lives in the narrow rectangle \(\alpha\in[-1/2,\,2]\), \(\beta\approx0\) (lower bound causality, upper bound Ostrogradsky). "Why this rectangle?" is the wall this series reached.

What we found was not "what constants are" but "the axis of order"
Part VIII
Differentiating the effective constants
We kept saying \(c\) is defined and cannot be differentiated. But what about an effective speed of light? ── a reader's question restarted the series.
Episode 16 out restart

The effective speed of light ── even a defined value can be differentiated

In wave language the speed of light is the slope of a dispersion relation, \(d\omega/dk\). So "differentiating it" means taking the second derivative of that relation.
From the Sellmeier equation for fused silica we obtained the zero-material-dispersion wavelength 1272.8 nm (published about 1270 nm) ── optical fibre uses 1310 nm because the second derivative of the refractive index vanishes there. In gravity, \(c_{\rm eff}=c(1+2\Phi/c^2)\), which integrates to the Shapiro delay (262 µs round trip for Cassini).
The group velocity exceeds \(c\) and goes negative, yet \(n(\infty)=1\) holds to 8 digits ── ★ information never exceeds \(c\). And what changes is always a dimensionless quantity.

"Effective" is the signal that a dimensionful quantity is being rewritten as a dimensionless one
Episode 17 out ★ unification

Effective mass ── the reciprocal of mass was a curvature

Expand the dispersion relation at \(k=0\) and ── ★ mass at order 0, speed at order 1, mass again at order 2. One word pointing at different features of one curve.
Tight binding gives \(m^*=\hbar^2/(2ta^2)\) (matching numerical differentiation to \(10^{-8}\)), and at the top of a band the mass goes negative (holes). Real materials span 0.014 to 200 ── four orders.
★ Graphene is a world of zero mass and effective light speed \(c/300\), where \(\alpha_{\text{graphene}}=2.19\) ── where light is slow, electromagnetism is strong. And the photon in a superconductor (5.06 eV in niobium) is bound to the W boson by one formula.

"Mass" is a name for a way of reading a dispersion relation
Episode 18 out ★ a finding

Where does mass come from? ── put it in a box, or soak it in a medium

Of the proton's 938.27 MeV, the quark masses sum to 8.99 MeV ── 0.96 %. 99 % of your body weight is the energy of a confined field.
In wave language it is simple ── a wave in a box has a lowest frequency (\(\pi\hbar c/L\) is 620 MeV at 1 fm). The other source is coupling to a condensate, and inverting the Yukawa couplings gives \(y_{\rm top}=0.9919\) alone of order one, with five and a half orders down to the electron.
★ And confinement and condensate are the same thing in wave language ── both create an intercept in a dispersion relation, one with boundaries and one with a background field. The photon in a waveguide, the photon in a superconductor, the proton and the electron all acquire mass the same way.

There are only two ways to make an intercept: boundaries, or a background field
Episode 19 out ★ a finding

A decay constant is an imaginary frequency ── so no full period is needed

Episode 3 said a period longer than the age of the universe is indistinguishable from a constant. So what about \(^{232}\)Th's half-life of 14.05 Gyr?
★ The answer ── one gram gives 4057 counts a second; one second is enough. Because \(e^{-\lambda t}=e^{i(i\lambda)t}\) ── a decay constant lives on the imaginary axis. ★ The real axis has phase and the imaginary axis does not, so a time average can be traded for an ensemble average.
The optimal dating nuclide has \(T_{1/2}=4.78\) Gyr and \(^{238}\)U sits at 0.935 of it. Fully ionised \(^{187}\)Re decays \(10^9\) times faster. And the weak force is weak not from its coupling (\(\alpha_W\) is 4.3× stronger) but from the cutoff \(M_W\).

The "longer than the universe" wall applies only to oscillations
Episode 20 out ★ a finding

The \(c\cdot t=\)const coordinate system ── the wave equation became the critical \(1/r^2\)

Define \(c(t)=R/t\) and the speed of light is differentiable (\(\dot c/c=-1/t=-H\)). Read as a physical change it is excluded by a factor of seven million against the bound on \(\alpha\).
But as a wave ── the phase goes as \(\ln t\) (every wave is a hyperbolic chirp) and the mode equation becomes \(\ddot\psi+(A^2/t^2)\psi=0\). ★ Episode 13's critical \(1/r^2\), word for word.
In logarithmic time it reads \(f''+(A^2-1/4)f=0\) ── a plain oscillator (12–16 digits). And criticality at \(A=1/2\) is horizon crossing, outside which \(s=0,1\) give a constant mode and a growing mode. ★ The \(c\cdot t=\)const system was time measured logarithmically.

In a universe whose basic symmetry is scale, the natural tool is the imaginary order
Part IX
Information as waves
Integrate a bit rate and you get information. So information has a ladder of orders too.
Episode 21 out ★ convergence

Integrate a bit rate and you get information ── and information lives on the imaginary axis

Shannon's formula \(I=BT\log_2(1+S/N)\) is "bandwidth × time" ── ★ Episode 9's bandwidth theorem itself. The telephone line's 30.9 kbps was the dial-up wall, and optical fibre is nine orders faster because its band is nine orders wider.
Landauer's limit is 0.0179 eV/bit at room temperature (semiconductors are still \(10^4\) above), the speed bound is \(5.4\times10^{50}\) /s for 1 kg, the capacity bound \(9\times10^{44}\) bit for a human ── all restatements of "energy sets bandwidth".
★ The universe's information goes as \(I\propto(ct)^2\), so it differentiates to \(1.4\times10^{105}\) bit/s with constant information acceleration. ★ So the \(c\cdot t=\)const system also holds the universe's information constant. And information lives on the imaginary axis ── "the arrow of time" is the imaginary axis of the complex frequency plane.

The limits on information were all the bandwidth theorem in disguise
Episode 22 out ★ settled

Measurement as a wave ── observing is choosing a basis to project onto

Measure the same wave in "time" or in "frequency" ── that choice is all the observer gets, and with a Gaussian window \(\Delta t\cdot\Delta\omega\) is always 0.5 (Episode 9's Gabor limit).
★ And the wavelet transform turns out to have uniform resolution on the \(\ln\omega\) axis (\(\Delta\omega/\omega\) constant, verified numerically) ── the same family as Episode 13's imaginary order and Episode 20's logarithmic time. If Fourier is the tool of time translation, wavelets are the tool of scale transformation.
★ Episode 4's homework is settled too ── a step change can be caught with wavelets (the transition width scales with \(\Delta t\)), but transition width × \(\Delta\omega\) stays fixed. Stationarity can be dropped; uncertainty cannot. Decoherence is \(V=e^{-\sigma_\varphi^2/2}\) ── ★ monotone, hence a phenomenon of the imaginary axis.

Only the split is yours; measurement is irreversible because phase is thrown onto the imaginary axis
Part X
Breaking the assumptions
Break the assumption this series has carried throughout ── linearity. What breaks, and what survives?
Episode 23 out ★ a finding

Non-linearity ── where the word "filter" stops working

"Filter" and "transfer function" all presuppose superposition. Add a squared term and ── two frequencies in, seven out (DC, difference, sum, second harmonics). Something no linear filter can ever do.
In a Duffing oscillator the same frequency and the same drive give two different answers (a gap of up to 1.85), so a transfer function cannot even be defined as a function. Measured as distortion, the everyday "linear world" is an approximation that ignores about 0.1 % ── linearity is not a property of nature but a consequence of smallness.
★★ And yet every constraint from Part IV survives (causality, the bandwidth theorem, the arrow of time, dimensional analysis) ── because "filter" is a word of linearity, while the three constraints come from time running one way. Sperm live at \(Re\sim10^{-4}\), completely linear ── hence the scallop theorem.

What broke was the language of linearity; what survived came from the imaginary axis
Episode 24 out ★ synthesis

Gravitational waves ── radiation one order higher

Here what waves is spacetime itself, and the measured quantity \(h=\Delta L/L\) is dimensionless from the start (Episode 7 as a definition). In GW150914 a 4 km arm moved by a thousandth of a proton.
The monopole dies by mass conservation and the dipole by momentum conservation, so radiation starts at the quadrupole. ★ Electromagnetic is the second derivative of a dipole squared; gravitational is the third derivative of a quadrupole squared ── one order higher.
The reference power is \(c^5/G=3.6\times10^{52}\) W (built from \(G\) and \(c\) alone). The flux at Earth was 3 mW/m² ── the order of moonlight, yet the arm moved a thousandth of a proton. The chirp goes as \(\tau^{-3/8}\), ★ differing from Episode 13's \(1/t\) because there is a special moment: the merger. The 45° polarisations follow from spin 2 ＝ a rank-2 tensor.

Spin, multipole order and polarisation angle are three names for one thing
Episode 25 out ★ a finding

The strong force as waves ── confinement was not a box but a string

Plot hadron masses as \(M^2\) against \(J\) and they fall on a clean straight line (a Regge trajectory), whose slope gives ★ a string tension of 14.4 tonnes-force. Constant at any distance ── which is why quarks can never be separated. The string's ladder has the same structure as Episode 6's waveguide and Episode 18's box.
The number of colours is measurable directly as the dimensionless R ratio (\(N_c=3\) fits all three bands), agreeing independently with the tau lifetime of Episode 19.
★★ And inverting asymptotic freedom ── a dimensionful \(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\) is born from the dimensionless \(\alpha_s\) alone (dimensional transmutation). The corresponding length is 0.94 fm ── the size of a proton. ★ 99 % of the mass around you is a dimensionless quantity that acquired a dimension. And gluons are massless yet reach only 1 fm ── Episode 23's non-linearity changes the shape of the field itself.

Dimensionful quantities are built out of dimensionless ones
Part XI
Settling the hypothesis
Grade the hypothesis made in Episode 2 with 25 episodes’ worth of tools. Having declared it falsifiable, go looking for the refutation.
Episode 26 new ★ Settled

Is a fractional order always the result of coarse-graining? ── we found a counterexample

Stack 1000 stages of a ladder made only of resistors and capacitors (both integer order) and the phase is \(-44.80^\circ\) with a slope of \(-9.98\) dB/dec ── ★ a half order (matching the exact semi-infinite solution). The reverse holds too: any fractional order with \(0<\alpha<1\) can be written as a superposition of first-order relaxations (\(g(\tau)\propto\tau^{\alpha-1}\)).
★★ And yet ── keep only the end mass of a uniform spring chain and you get a pure resistance at phase 0°. Infinitely many degrees of freedom removed, and still integer order. "Coarse-graining always gives a fractional order" is ★ refuted by counterexample.
The difference was how the removed degrees of freedom were distributed. With a bath of spectral density \(J(\omega)\propto\omega^s\) the order is exactly \(s\). ★ Rewritten hypothesis: a fractional order appears when the coarse-grained degrees of freedom are distributed scale-invariantly. Together with Episode 13 (imaginary order) and Episode 22 (wavelets) ── all three turned on scale invariance.

A fractional order is the trace of scale-invariant degrees of freedom that were removed
Episode 27 new ★ Discovery

Where does log-uniform come from? ── what nature had ready was the exponential

The last mystery from last time gets its answer. ★ Making 1/f needs no power-law distribution ── with \(\tau=\tau_0e^{E/kT}\), \(\ln\tau\) is linear in the barrier \(E\), so \(g(\tau)=kT\,D(E)/\tau\). A flat \(D\) alone gives \(1/\tau\). At room temperature a decade costs 0.0595 eV, so 0.36 eV of scatter buys 6 decades of 1/f.
★★ And why the exponent pins near 1 came out too ── \(\beta-1\) = (decades over which \(D\) varies) ÷ (decades over which \(\tau\) spreads). Even a 1000× variation gives \(\beta-1=0.25\). The 0.8–1.4 scatter of measurements is exactly this ratio. ★ 1/f is not fine-tuning but a phenomenon hard to avoid. In \(\alpha\) decay, \(Q\) varies by 2.19× while the half-life varies by 24.2 decades (Geiger–Nuttall, \(R^2=0.9955\)).
★ And one prediction failed ── discrete scale invariance (the Efimov ratio 22.69) leaves a 2.95 dB log-periodic ripple (matching \(2/\cosh(\pi^2/\ln\lambda)\) to 3 digits). What was crushed in Episode 13 was the imaginary-order impulse response ── a different thing. Turned around this is testable: 1/f flat to 0.1 dB means a ladder ratio of at most 4.5.

The origin of fractional order was the exponential function

The overall answer

"Are all physical constants integrals of something?" ──
Half right, and the right half sits at the centre of particle physics.

The right half: couplings are the integral of a beta function. \(1/\alpha\) runs from 137.036 to 128.940.
The wrong half: dimensionful constants cannot be integrated into. They turn into unit conventions.

And the same "mass" succeeded completely one way and failed completely the other ── as a wave it is a cutoff frequency (giving Yukawa and \(E=mc^2\)); as an integral it is refuted.

Hits are dimensionless, misses are dimensionful.

And what Part IV onwards revealed is that "the order" was this series' real protagonist ── causality, uncertainty, the arrow of time, why heat does not travel, why the sky is blue, all of it. In Episode 13 that order became a plane; in Episode 14 it sorted into "three ways to leave the integers"; in Episode 15 it folded into "physics lives in a narrow rectangle".
