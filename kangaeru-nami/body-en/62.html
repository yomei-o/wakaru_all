Fusion and superconductivity turned out to be the same integral

The things that tunnel ──

The core of §02

★ It is not the average particle that reacts. The product of the Maxwell tail (\(e^{-E/kT}\)) and the sharply rising tunnelling probability (\(e^{-\sqrt{E_G/E}}\)) makes a Gamow peak, and only that burns.
★★ Even at the Sun’s centre (1.35 keV = 15.7 million K) the peak is at 6.1 keV ── only the tail, 4.5× the average.
★★★ The reactivity matches the literature to within 1 %: \(\langle\sigma v\rangle_{dt}=1.089\times10^{-22}\) at 10 keV (lit. \(1.1\times10^{-22}\)) and \(4.244\times10^{-22}\) at 20 keV (lit. \(4.3\times10^{-22}\)) m³/s; the peak is at 66.5 keV (lit. ≈64).

The core of §04 ── why single atoms are visible

★ At \(\phi=4.5\) eV (a metal work function), moving the tip 1 Å closer multiplies the current by 8.8 ── exactly 0.94 decades.
★★★ The STM rule of thumb “one decade per ångström” is this formula itself, and it is why a single atom’s corrugation is visible.
★★ A Josephson junction (AlOx, \(\phi\approx2\) eV) costs 6.3 decades at 1 nm; another 0.5 nm of oxide costs 3 more ── which is why superconducting qubit fabrication demands ångström control.
★ The Coulomb barrier goes as \(E^{-1/2}\), but a square barrier is linear in \(d\) ── so with STM, decades can be bought linearly in distance. In Episode 54’s terms, it is the most generous counterpart there is.

05★★★ Cold fusion ── counting how many decades are missing

The barrier-lowering effect of electrons is captured experimentally by a single screening energy \(U_e\) (\(\sigma_{\text{scr}}(E)=\sigma_{\text{bare}}(E+U_e)\)). Inside a bound molecule \(E\approx0\), so effectively \(E\to U_e\).

Cold fusion reduces to this one line $$\lambda_{\text{pair}}=\frac{A_{dd}}{V_{\text{mol}}}\exp\!\left(-\sqrt{\frac{E_G}{U_e}}\right),\qquad E_G(dd)=986\ \text{keV}$$

★★ Calibrating this model on a single point (\(U_e=36\) eV reproduces Koonin–Nauenberg’s \(3\times10^{-64}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) for \(\mathrm{D_2}\)) automatically yields \(10^{9.7}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) for the muonic molecule (literature ≈\(10^9\)) ── one calibration point spanning 73 orders of magnitude to within a factor of a few. So the model is usable.

The core of §05 ── this is the genuinely interesting part

★ Experimental upper limits are \(10^{-24}\) to \(10^{-23}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) per pair (Jones 1989 and others). The \(U_e\) corresponding to that is 194 eV.
★★★ Yet the measured \(U_e\) for deuterated Pd is 250 eV ── above the limit.
★★ Taken at face value, the model therefore predicts that cold fusion should be observed. It is not.
★★★ So the conclusion is not “cold fusion does not exist” but “extrapolating \(U_e\) down to \(E\to0\) breaks the approximation” ── in Episode 55’s language, a conditional value being used outside its condition.

06★★★ “Does the wave picture give anything?” ── three honest counts

First the premise. Treating fusion as a wave is exactly what Gamow did in 1928, and that in itself is not new ── tunnelling is a wave leaking where classical particles cannot go. With that said, here are three things the wave language turns into something countable.

(A) d+t is chosen not for its barrier but for a resonance

★ \(B_G\) is 2.99 larger for d+t ── tunnelling is 1.53× worse for it.
★★★ And yet the cross-section is 120× larger. The whole difference lives in \(S(E)\) (182×).
★★ d+t’s advantage is the ⁵He resonance at 64 keV ── a standing wave state on the nuclear side. “Reactors use d+t because its barrier is lower” is, strictly, backwards.

(B) Is the muon “just barely” heavy enough?

Replace the electron with a particle of mass ratio \(x\): the molecule shrinks to \(1/x\), \(U_e\) grows by \(x\), and \(G\) shrinks by \(1/\sqrt{x}\).

★★★ The threshold for useful catalysis is a mass ratio of about 85. The muon sits at 195.7, a factor 2.3 above ── not marginal.
★★ But there is no charged particle between the electron and the muon. The only charged particles above the threshold are the muon and the pion, and the pion decays in 26 ns.
★ So muon-catalysed fusion has exactly one candidate ── the muon is the only particle in nature satisfying both mass and lifetime. And what sets that threshold is the \(x^{-1/2}\) law of leakage.

(C) ★★★ If it is a wave, it should add coherently ── does that close the gap?

This is the strongest thing this episode can say. Claims of the form “cold fusion is explained by a collective wave effect” can be judged without looking inside the model at all, by counting.

The core of §06

★★★ Applying the largest conceivable coherence — perfect superradiance — to every deuteron in Earth’s oceans still falls 9 orders short.
★★ So the “waves add coherently” rescue fails for any specific model whatsoever ── it can be refuted by counting.
★ But this is not a proof that cold fusion does not exist. It is the narrower claim that a coherence mechanism cannot explain it, and the only road left is ① from §05.

Exercises

  1. §04 says STM buys decades linearly in distance. What is that in the language of Episodes 53 and 54?
    Show answer
    The most generous way of buying range there is.
    ★ Episode 53: “range can only be bought logarithmically” (ten times the observation \(T\) buys one decade). ★★ But a square barrier has \(G\propto d\), so shrinking the distance linearly buys decades linearly ── one decade per ångström.
    ★★★ The Coulomb barrier has \(G\propto E^{-1/2}\), so doubling the decades needs four times the energy ── that side belongs to Episode 53’s “only as a power”.
    ★★ So “what you must buy to gain decades” is decided by the barrier’s shape ── STM reaching atomic resolution while fusion demands 100 million K is only a difference of exponent inside the same formula.
  2. §06 (C) says the claim can be refuted by counting. Why is that a strong argument?
    Show answer
    Because it does not depend on the mechanism at all.
    ★ The usual rebuttal knocks down one proposed mechanism at a time, and has to start over whenever a new one appears.
    ★★ But coherent amplification is bounded by \(N\) for any mechanism (even superradiance gives \(N\)). So counting the required \(N\) against the available \(N\) ends it.
    ★★★ This is the strongest form of Episode 55’s “count the assumptions” ── judging by the ceiling of what an assumption could deliver, without examining the assumption.
    ★ Its limits are equally sharp: it says nothing about non-coherence mechanisms (a genuinely different screening, say). ★★ Unless you write down what it does and does not refute, this argument gets abused.
  3. §05 finds the measured \(U_e\) exceeding the limit. Is that a contradiction?
    Show answer
    Not a contradiction ── a broken extrapolation.
    ★ \(U_e\) is obtained by measuring how fast \(\sigma\) rises at \(E=5\)–20 keV. ★★ There \(U_e/E\sim0.02\), and the shift \(E\to E+U_e\) is a correct first-order approximation.
    ★★★ But at \(E\to0\), \(U_e/E\to\infty\) ── we are five decades outside where that first-order form applies.
    ★ In Episode 57’s language: this is an edge. The leading term of an expansion was carried outside the range where the expansion works.
    ★★ In Episode 60’s language: a value was used without saying where it was cut. \(U_e\) is “the \(U_e\) measured at 5–20 keV”, not a constant.

Summary — one integral, counting 83 decades

“Superconductivity and fusion are all just tunnelling, aren’t they?” ── Yes.

★★★ \(G=\dfrac{2}{\hbar}\displaystyle\int\sqrt{2m(V-E)}\,dr\), \(\;\)decades \(=G/\ln 10\).
Only \(V(r)\) changes.

★ All 18 checks pass: \(B_G\) agrees with the literature to four figures, \(\langle\sigma v\rangle_{dt}\) to within 1 %, ²³⁸U’s \(\alpha\) decay gives \(\log_{10}(T/\mathrm{s})=17.16\) against a measured 17.15, STM gives 0.94 decades per ångström, and dd-μ gives \(5.2\times10^9\) against a literature \(1.5\times10^9\).

★★ Cold fusion reduces to \(U_e\) alone. In a model calibrated on one \(\mathrm{D_2}\) point that reproduces the muonic molecule 73 orders away, the \(U_e\) matching the experimental limit is 194 eV ── while deuterated Pd measures 250 eV. ★★★ So the conclusion is not “cold fusion does not exist” but “extrapolating \(U_e\) to \(E\to0\) is broken” ── a question that can be measured.

★★★ And three things the wave picture yielded. (A) d+t’s advantage is not the barrier but the ⁵He resonance (its \(B_G\) is in fact 1.53× worse; the difference is entirely \(S(E)\)). (B) The catalysis threshold is a mass ratio of 85, and the only charged particles above it are the muon and the pion, the latter dying in 26 ns ── hence exactly one candidate. (C) A coherence mechanism falls 9 orders short even with every deuteron in Earth’s oceans in perfect superradiance.

★ (C) is the strongest statement here ── an entire class of explanations refuted by counting, without inspecting a single mechanism. ★★ But what was refuted is “coherence mechanisms”, not “cold fusion”. Without writing that distinction down, the argument gets abused (Exercise 2).
