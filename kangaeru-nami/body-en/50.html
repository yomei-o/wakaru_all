The Map —
Fifty episodes on, where did we arrive? Episode 1's question was one line — "Differentiate a sound and it goes high, integrate it and it goes low. So what does the space in between — a fractional order — mean?"
★ Fifty episodes later: the order \(\alpha\) turned out to be the dB/oct, the phase, the dimension, the Hurst exponent, the AdS mass and the structure-function exponent. One and the same number.
★★ And honestly — no formula unknown to humanity came out of it.

Exercises

  1. §06 says no new formula came out. Were the fifty episodes wasted, then?
    Show answer
    Not wasted — but not a "discovery" either. As Episode 43 put it, re-shelving does three things — ① you notice when the same thing appears twice ② you notice what is missing ③ you learn which quantities are sensitive.
    ★ ③ is the most practical thing this series produced — Episode 42's "intermittency moves the dB/oct by only 0.081," Episode 47's "\(\phi^2\) passes on \(n_s\) but is excluded by \(r\)." Both let you say, before measuring, whether the measurement is worth doing.
    ★★ But that is organizing existing knowledge, not new physics. ★ Continuing to say so is the thing this series guarded most carefully.
  2. §04 stresses "zero dependencies." Why is that a policy?
    Show answer
    Because "the calculation can be reproduced" is very nearly this series' only guarantee. No experiments were performed here; where literature values were used, they are labelled as such.
    ★ What remains is only "do the stated numbers really follow from the stated formulas?" — and that had to be checkable by anyone.
    ★★ Depending on a library means depending on its version — a calculation that will not run in ten years guarantees nothing. ★ That is why the FFT, the Jacobi method and the complex gamma function were each written where needed (13,966 lines in total).
  3. Of §05's unfinished list, which should be taken up next, and why?
    Show answer
    The first line: handling noisy real data. Three reasons —
    ★ ① The tools already exist: Episode 49's handbook works on noiseless functions; only the statistics need adding.
    ★ ② The payoff is large: put error bars on "a power law over how many decades," and every claim since Episode 27 becomes quantitative.
    ★ ③ It is falsifiable: applied to real data, the handbook's failures will show up immediately. Which fits the rule set in Episode 15 — having declared the hypotheses falsifiable, go looking for falsifications.
    ★★ The furthest, conversely, is the cosmological constant problem: these tools handle only differences, so it is out of reach in principle (Episode 45 §08).

Summary — the map

Episode 1's \(\text{dB/oct}=6\alpha\) and \(\text{phase}=90\alpha\) were still the axis 49 episodes later. Aliases kept arriving — dimension \(d=2\alpha+3\), Hurst \(H=\alpha-\frac12\), AdS's \(\nu=\alpha/2\), spectral index \(\beta=2\alpha\) — which is what made "turbulence is 5/6-order" sayable.

The order leaves the integers for five reasons (superposition, dimension, criticality, bulk mass, distribution), and the axis ends in three ways (integers, distributions, no power law).

All 28 numbers were recomputed and checked (zero FAILs) — though that only guarantees internal consistency; what guards against contradicting nature is the list of failed predictions.

Counting the series itself — 49 episodes, 49 body files in each language, 48 verification scripts, 13,966 lines, zero dependencies. FFT, Jacobi, complex gamma: each written where needed, because reproducibility is very nearly the only guarantee here.
