Thinking in Waves Episode 42 / Part XIII — The Frontier, as Waves

When the order stops being a single number

Intermittency in Turbulence —
The order was a distribution Kolmogorov's \(-5/3\), in this series' language, makes turbulence a "5/6-order" wave — 5 dB/oct, phase 75°. Exactly between 1/f (order 1/2) and Brownian motion (order 1).
★★ But measurements depart from it systematically, and the departure is this — the order is not one number but a distribution of width 1.222.
★ And the mean does not move: \(\zeta_3=1\) stays exactly 1 no matter how intermittent the flow.

02If the order is one number, every exponent agrees

We synthesize \(f^{-\beta}\) noise and measure the exponents of the structure functions \(S_p(r)=\langle|\delta u|^p\rangle\propto r^{\zeta_p}\) for each \(p\). With a single order, \(\zeta_p/p\) must not depend on \(p\).

03But real turbulence splits the dissipation multiplicatively

Energy passes from large eddies to small ones, but never evenly. In a model that splits it into \(p\) and \(1-p\) at each stage (the p-model), we measure the \(q\)-th moment \(Z(q,r)=\sum\mu_i^q\propto r^{\tau(q)}\).

The point of §03

Measurement agrees with the exact result to \(10^{-14}\) (\(p=0.7\), 18 levels = 262144 boxes).

★★ At \(p=0.5\) (an even split) it returns to the straight line \(\tau(q)=q-1\) (confirmed numerically: 0, 1.000000, 3.000000). The bend comes from splitting unevenly.

\(\tau_\varepsilon(1)=1-1+\tau(1)=0\) regardless of \(p\) (mean dissipation is conserved) — so \(\zeta_3=1\) exactly, for every \(p\). That is the 4/5 law.

★ Which is why intermittency is nearly invisible in a power spectrum. "\(-5/3\) fits well" is really the second moment being insensitive.

Exercises

  1. §06 explained why "\(-5/3\) fits well." Does that mean K41 was right?
    Show answer
    No — it means the second moment was insensitive. The shift in \(\zeta_2\) is only 0.027, or 0.081 dB/oct. It is buried in measurement error.
    ★ Where K41 is wrong shows up clearly in the sixth moment — predicted 2.000 against a measured 1.78, an 11 % discrepancy.
    ★★ The lesson: seeing one exponent fit well is not a test of a theory. This is why every episode of this series records which moment was measured. ★ The same structure as Episode 5's "anything longer than the age of the universe is indistinguishable from a constant" — through an insensitive quantity, a difference is the same as no difference.
  2. In §04, \(\alpha\) spanned 0.51 to 1.74. Why no negative \(\alpha\), and none above 2?
    Show answer
    Because each stage offers only two splits, \(p\) and \(1-p\). The strongest point is the one that drew \(p\) every time, with \(\mu=p^n\), i.e. \(\alpha=-\log_2p\); the weakest is \(\alpha=-\log_2(1-p)\).
    ★ So the ends of the distribution are set by the most extreme path. Change the model and the ends change — a log-normal cascade formally has no ends.
    ★★ In real turbulence, though, the cascade stops at the viscous (Kolmogorov) length, so it cannot become arbitrarily extreme. ★ As in Episodes 5 and 22 — how finely you look decides the answer.
  3. Episode 2's 1/f was also a superposition. How does it differ from intermittency here?
    Show answer
    Addition versus multiplication. Episode 2 added many relaxation processes to make \(1/f\) — additive superposition tends to a Gaussian by the central limit theorem, and the order comes out as a single number.
    ★ Here each stage multiplies. A multiplicative superposition adds in the logarithm, so it tends to a log-normal — heavy tails, where rare large values dominate. Hence the bending of high moments.
    ★★ Whether the order is one number or a distribution was decided by whether the superposition adds or multiplies. ★ Episode 28 sorted "two sources of exponentials," and one of them — additive becoming multiplicative — is at work here too.

Summary — the order was a distribution

Kolmogorov's \(-5/3\) comes out of one line of dimensional analysis. Translated: turbulence is a "5/6-order" wave — 5 dB/oct, phase 75°, Hurst \(H=1/3\). Exactly between 1/f (order 1/2) and Brownian motion (order 1).

First we checked what a single order looks like — for synthetic \(f^{-\beta}\) noise, \(\zeta_p/p\) is constant in \(p\) and equals \((\beta-1)/2\).

But a real cascade multiplies. In the p-model our measured \(\tau(q)\) matches the exact result to \(10^{-14}\), and \(\tau(q)\) is not linear in \(q\) (2.010 at \(q=4\), against 3.000 for a monofractal). At \(p=0.5\) it straightens out — the bend comes from splitting unevenly.

★★★ The core — after a Legendre transform, \(\alpha\) runs
from 0.514573 to 1.736966 (width \(\log_2\frac{1-p}{p}=1.222\)).
The order is not one number but a set with width.

In terms of velocity structure functions, \(\zeta_p\) bends below \(p/3\) — and one dial (\(p=0.7\)) reproduces the literature values of \(\zeta_1\) through \(\zeta_6\) to 1–2 % (0.361/0.37, 0.694/0.70, 1.000/1.00, 1.282/1.28, 1.543/1.53, 1.786/1.78).

★ And yet \(\zeta_3=1\) does not move — mean dissipation is conserved, so \(\tau_\varepsilon(1)=0\) and \(\zeta_3=1\) exactly, for every \(p\) (the 4/5 law).
★★★ The crucial part: even at \(p=0.7\), \(\beta=1.6937\), differing from K41 by only 0.081 dB/oct. "\(-5/3\) fits well" was really the second moment being insensitive.

★ After Episode 14's three and Episode 39's fourth — ⑤ the order is not a single number. The act of "measuring dB/oct" depends on which moment you measure with. ★ The single axis carried since Episode 1 stops being single here.

Kolmogorov's \(-5/3\) law and \(\zeta_p=p/3\), the 4/5 law (\(\zeta_3=1\)), Mandelbrot's multiplicative cascades and the p-model, \(\tau(q)=-\log_2(p^q+(1-p)^q)\), the multifractal formalism (the Legendre transform), the refined similarity hypothesis, and the measured values of \(\zeta_p\) (such as \(\zeta_6\approx1.78\)) are all well-known results and none are discoveries of this article — what this article did was place them on this series' axis of "order," add "the order becomes a distribution" as a fifth entry to Episode 14's list, and put a number on how little intermittency moves the dB/oct: 0.081. In §05, \(p=0.7\) reproduces the literature well, but that is one fitted parameter and does not make the p-model the correct model. Several models of intermittency exist (log-normal, She–Lévêque and others) and this article simply took the simplest. The \(\beta=1.2\) row in §02 is off by 0.06 because of a limitation of the synthesis method.
