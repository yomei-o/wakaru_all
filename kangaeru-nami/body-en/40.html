The point of §02

The drive is at \(\omega=2\), the answer at \(\omega_0=1\) — exactly half. The growth rate matches \(h\omega_0/4\) to five digits.
★ It is the same mechanism as pumping a swing: raise and lower your weight twice per swing and the motion grows. Input at twice the frequency, answer at half.
★★ Note that linearity was not given up here — the equation is linear in \(x\). What was given up is time-invariance.

The point of §04

Above \(h_{\rm th}=2\gamma/\omega_0=0.1\) the oscillation starts, and the phase falls into one of two states \(\pi\) apart (measured 3.141593 rad).
★ "There are two" is the symmetry breaking — shift time by one drive period and one turns into the other. The system picked one \(\mathbb Z_2\) branch by itself.

05★★ Rigidity — mis-set it, and does it come back?

Here is the real question. If you only want a half, §02 already did it. A time crystal earns the word "phase" because the half survives a mis-set drive.

We test it with a classical spin chain. Each period: (a) precession about \(z\) by disordered Ising couplings, (b) a kick about \(x\) by the angle \(\pi(1-\varepsilon)\). With no interactions, the response should slide obediently to \((1-\varepsilon)/2\).

The point of §05 — ★★★ the core of this episode

Without interactions the peak slides obediently to \((1-\varepsilon)/2\).

★ That is rigidity. Mis-set the kick by 15 % and the system cancels its own error and returns to the half.
★★ Switch the interactions off and it vanishes — so rigidity is not a property of one oscillator but of many. That is exactly where it differs from Episode 23's subharmonic in a single nonlinear oscillator.

Exercises

  1. §02's Mathieu equation is linear in \(x\), yet a subharmonic appeared. Does that contradict Episode 1's "linear systems cannot create a frequency"?
    Show answer
    No. Episode 1's claim was about "linear and time-invariant."
    ★ The Mathieu equation is linear, but its coefficient is a function of time — it gave up time-invariance.
    ★★ To create a new frequency it is enough to give up one of linearity and time-invariance. Nonlinearity (Ep. 23) drops the former, parametric driving the latter. And a time crystal drops both, and must additionally be many-body.
  2. In §05 the interactions cancelled the error. By what mechanism?
    Show answer
    The kick error exactly cancels over two periods. With a kick of \(\pi(1-\varepsilon)\), each period falls short by an angle \(\pi\varepsilon\).
    ★ But the neighbours' \(z\) components also flip sign every period, so the precession reverses every period too — the shortfall comes back with the opposite sign next period and averages out over two.
    ★★ This is a self-correcting feedback loop (Episode 18's negative feedback). Hence "switch off the interactions and it disappears" — the feedback path is gone. ★ If \(\varepsilon\) is too large the feedback cannot keep up, and it collapses as in §06.
  3. §07 said equilibrium is impossible. Is §05's calculation then not in equilibrium?
    Show answer
    It is not. §05's spin chain is kicked every single period — work is done on it periodically from outside.
    ★ In fact a continuously kicked system usually heats to infinite temperature, where no order survives. That is why localization enters the time-crystal story: to stop the heating.
    ★★ In this classical calculation the disordered couplings play part of that role, but we did not verify that the heating actually stops (only 4096 periods were simulated). ★ So it is held as pending — per Episodes 29–30, what has not been checked does not become a claim.

Summary — what matters is not the half, but that it comes back

First we confirmed where it is not — feed \(f_0\) into a linear time-invariant system and \(f_0/2\) comes out at −305 dB (the rounding floor). Exactly as Episode 1 said, an LTI system cannot create a frequency.

Wobble the coefficient and it appears — the Mathieu equation \(\ddot x+\omega_0^2(1+h\cos2\omega_0t)x=0\) has a growth rate matching \(h\omega_0/4\) to five digits. In Floquet terms \(\det M=1.000000000\), and where \(\mathrm{tr}\,M\) drops below \(-2\), \(\mu\) passes through \(-1\) — sign flips in one period, returns in two (continuing Episode 36).

Add dissipation and a threshold \(h_{\rm th}=2\gamma/\omega_0\) appears; above it the phase falls into one of two states \(\pi\) apart (measured 3.141593 rad) — "there are two" is the broken symmetry; Episode 32 replayed in time.

★★★ The core — producing a half takes only the Mathieu equation.
A time crystal earns the word "phase" because you can mis-set the kick by 15 %
and the response still sits at exactly 0.500000.

And switch the interactions off and that stubbornness disappears — at \(J=0\) the peak slides obediently to \((1-\varepsilon)/2\) (0.490, 0.475, 0.450, 0.425, as predicted). ★ Rigidity is not a property of one oscillator but of many. The mechanism is negative feedback that cancels the error over two periods (Episode 18).

★ The width is not a single threshold — at \(\varepsilon=0.18\) the frequency still reads 0.4988, but the amplitude has already died first, 0.846 → 0.021. Clearly locked below \(\varepsilon\approx0.12\); clearly gone above \(\approx0.25\).

★ And it cannot exist in equilibrium — a passive system has \(\mathrm{Re}\,Z\ge0\) and cannot deliver energy of its own (Episodes 17, 18); starting requires both drive and dissipation.

★ Honestly: this is classical only, with no quantum many-body localization. We simulated 4096 periods; "does it last forever" cannot be settled numerically (the same limit as Episode 5).
