Thinking in Waves Episode 11 ／ Part V · Physical quantities as waves

Ground temperature and the skin of a wire obey the same equation

Heat does not travel as a wave ──
first order in time, second in space The diffusion equation's dispersion relation is \(\omega=-iDk^2\) ── purely imaginary. It does not propagate; it decays in place.
Heat it periodically, though, and a frequency-dependent depth appears ── 12 cm for day/night, 2.2 m for the seasons.
And at 7 m underground, the seasons are shifted by half a year.

Part IV gave us three constraints on waves. From here we look at individual physical quantities with that eye.
First, heat ── and we find that the everyday phrase "heat travels" is not physically right.

01The dispersion relation goes purely imaginary

Put a plane wave into the diffusion equation $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=D\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}\quad\xrightarrow{\ e^{i(kx-\omega t)}\ }\quad -i\omega=-Dk^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\omega=-iDk^2}$$
EquationDispersion relationType of \(\omega\)Behaviour
Wave equation\(\omega=ck\)realpropagates without decay
Klein–Gordon\(\omega^2=c^2k^2+\omega_c^2\)real (above cutoff)propagates (Episode 6)
Diffusion equation\(\omega=-iDk^2\)★ purely imaginarydoes not propagate; decays in place

Conclusion of §01

The everyday phrase "heat travels" is not physically correct.
Heat does not propagate. It merely spreads and thins out with time.

02But "heat it periodically" changes the story

Drive the surface at frequency \(\omega\) (day/night, seasons, alternating current) and you fix \(\omega\) real and solve for \(k\).

Penetration depth $$k^2=\frac{i\omega}{D}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac{1+i}{\delta},\qquad \delta=\sqrt{\frac{2D}{\omega}}$$ $$T(x,t)=T_0\,e^{-x/\delta}\cos\!\left(\omega t-\frac{x}{\delta}\right)$$

The heart of §02

Real and imaginary parts come out equal in size. Therefore ──
at depth \(\delta\) the amplitude falls to \(1/e\) and the phase lags by exactly one radian.

★ "Decay" and "lag" always happen over the same length. That is diffusion's fingerprint.

The heart of §03

At a depth of \(\pi\delta=7.0\) m the seasons are shifted by half a year.
Seven metres down it is warmest in winter and coldest in summer.

★ Traditional ice houses and root cellars work because of this phase lag.
Both effects matter — the amplitude has fallen to \(1/23\), and the phase is half a year off.

04The very same equation appears in electromagnetism

Inside a good conductor, Maxwell's equations become a diffusion equation.

The skin effect $$\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\mu\sigma}\nabla^2\mathbf{B},\qquad D_{\text{em}}=\frac{1}{\mu\sigma}=\frac{\rho}{\mu}$$

★ Word for word the heat-conduction form. Only \(D\) differs

Copper's "electromagnetic diffusivity" is \(2.7\times10^4\) times soil's thermal one

\(D_{\text{em}}=\rho/\mu=1.34\times10^{-2}\) m²/s versus \(5\times10^{-7}\) m²/s for soil.
So electromagnetic fields act shallowly and fast ── which is why high-frequency conductors can be hollow, and why gold plating on a connector works.
★ And it comes out of exactly the same equation as the temperature of the ground.

05What is happening, in the language of calculus

The heart of §05

First order in time and second in space gives \(\omega\propto k^2\), making \(\omega\) purely imaginary.
The origin of "does not propagate" was a mismatch of orders.

And the Schrödinger equation has exactly the same combination of orders, yet one \(i\) makes it reversible and lets it propagate as a wave ──
★ which is why the diffusion equation is called "the Schrödinger equation in imaginary time". Indeed \(t\to-it\) maps one onto the other.

06So can heat be made to travel as a wave? ── yes

Add a second-order time derivative and the equation becomes hyperbolic (Cattaneo).

The Cattaneo equation $$\tau\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial t^2}+\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}=D\nabla^2T$$ $$\omega\tau\gg1:\ \omega\approx\pm k\sqrt{D/\tau}\ (\text{real ── a wave})\qquad \omega\tau\ll1:\ \omega\approx-iDk^2\ (\text{diffusion})$$
MediumRelaxation time \(\tau\)Cutoff \(1/\tau\)Second-sound speed \(\sqrt{D/\tau}\)
Metal (electrons)\(10^{-11}\) s\(1.6\times10^{10}\) Hz3162 m/s

The heart of §06 ── a cutoff pointing the other way from Episode 6

At the cutoff \(\omega_c=1/\tau\), diffusion switches over to wave propagation.
Episode 6's mass was a low-frequency cutoff; this one turns into a wave at high frequency ── the opposite direction.

In ordinary matter the cutoff is \(10^{10}\sim10^{11}\) Hz ── out of everyday reach, so heat looks like something that diffuses.
But in superfluid helium \(\tau\) is \(10^6\) times longer and the cutoff is around 10 kHz. There, heat really does travel as a wave ── second sound, observed in 1944.

Exercises

  1. Give two reasons why a cellar is cool in summer and warm in winter.
    Show answer
    ① The amplitude has decayed (\(1/e\) at 2.24 m, \(1/23\) at 7 m) ── the temperature swing itself is small.
    ② The phase has lagged (half a year at 7 m) ── when the surface is in summer, the ground is at "last winter's temperature".
    A shallow cellar gets only ①; a deep one gets both. Go deeper than 7 m and the anti-phase breaks down again, leaving simply a constant temperature.
  2. Give the numbers for why a high-frequency conductor can be hollow.
    Show answer
    At 1 GHz copper's skin depth is 2.06 μm. Current flows only in about that thickness.
    For a 1 mm wire that is \(\approx4\delta/d=0.8\ \%\) of the cross-section ── 99 % of the copper inside is wasted.
    Hence waveguides may be hollow, and only the surface finish (and gold plating) matters.
  3. If "heat does not travel", why is it hot near a fire?
    Show answer
    Because that heat arrives by radiation and convection, not conduction.
    Radiation is an electromagnetic wave (\(\omega=ck\), real) and literally propagates ── at the speed of light. Convection is bulk motion of matter.
    This episode dealt only with conduction, and where conduction dominates (inside a metal rod, the ground, a circuit board) it really does "thin out rather than travel".
    ★ Of the three modes of heat transfer, only conduction obeys the diffusion equation.

Summary　Whether it travels or thins out is set by the orders

The diffusion equation's dispersion relation is \(\omega=-iDk^2\) ── purely imaginary. It does not propagate; it decays in place. "Heat travels" is not physically right.

Heat it periodically, though, and a penetration depth \(\delta=\sqrt{2D/\omega}\) appears. Real and imaginary parts are equal, so decay and phase lag always occur over the same length ── diffusion's fingerprint.

In soil, 12 cm for day/night and 2.2 m for the seasons. And at 7.04 m the seasons are half a year out of phase ── why ice houses and root cellars work.

The same equation appears in electromagnetism ── the skin effect. Replace \(D\) by \(\rho/\mu\): copper gives 9.2 mm at 50 Hz and 2.1 μm at 1 GHz. The same mathematics as the temperature of the ground.

Irreversibility originates in the mismatch: first order in time, second in space. With the same orders but an \(i\), it becomes reversible (Schrödinger) ── the diffusion equation is "Schrödinger in imaginary time".

And add a second-order time term and heat turns into a wave above a cutoff frequency.
── In superfluid helium, that has actually been observed.

Next time Heat was the thing that does not travel. Next, the thing that certainly does ── electromagnetic waves.
And the question is: why does radiation require acceleration?
The answer is in the order ── only the far field picks up the second derivative. And from there comes the reason an electron does not fall into the nucleus.

On the numbers: the soil thermal diffusivity \(D=5\times10^{-7}\) m²/s is a representative value and varies by a factor of 2–3 with moisture and density (about \(2\times10^{-7}\) for dry sand, \(6\times10^{-7}\) for wet clay) ── so "2.2 m for the seasons" and "7 m for the half-year shift" carry the same spread. Real ground temperature also sits on a geothermal gradient (\(\approx25\) K/km) and is affected by snow cover and vegetation. The skin-depth formula is the good-conductor approximation (\(\sigma\gg\omega\varepsilon\)) and fails at high enough frequency. The relaxation time \(\tau\) in the Cattaneo equation is defined differently in different materials, and the table gives orders of magnitude ── the measured second-sound speed in superfluid helium is about 20 m/s, an order off our naive \(\sqrt{D/\tau}\) estimate of 3.2 m/s (it really needs the two-fluid model). ── To print, use your browser's Print → Save as PDF.
