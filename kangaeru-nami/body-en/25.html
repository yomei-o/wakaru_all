06★ Dimensional transmutation ── a mass born from a dimensionless number

Invert for the scale where the coupling diverges $$\frac{1}{\alpha_s(\mu)}=\frac{\beta_0}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\mu}{\Lambda}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Lambda=\mu\exp\Bigl(-\frac{2\pi}{\beta_0\alpha_s(\mu)}\Bigr)$$ $$\alpha_s(m_Z)=0.1179\ \Longrightarrow\ \Lambda_{\rm QCD}=87.3\ \text{MeV}\quad(\text{published}\approx210\ \text{MeV, two loops and beyond})$$

The heart of §06 ── the most important point of this episode

The only input is the dimensionless \(\alpha_s\).
And out comes a mass scale \(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\) with a dimension.

This is dimensional transmutation ── the most dramatic consequence of Episode 5's "constants are integrals over \(\ln\mu\)". The integration itself creates a new dimension.

The corresponding length is \(\hbar c/\Lambda\): 2.26 fm at one loop, but with the published \(\Lambda=210\) MeV it is 0.94 fm ── essentially the proton's charge radius, 0.84 fm.

The heart of §08

The \(\rho\)'s width is 19 % of its mass ── ★ it falls apart before completing one oscillation.
In Episode 9's language, \(\Delta\omega\) is the same order as \(\omega\) ── the limit of what can be counted as a wave.

The \(J/\psi\), by contrast, has \(\Gamma/M=3\times10^{-5}\) ── decaying strongly yet 6400 times narrower than the \(\rho\).
★ The reason is that its destination is closed off (OZI suppression) ── exactly the structure of Episode 19's \(^{187}\)Re. When the final-state modes are unavailable, the rate collapses.

Exercises

  1. In this episode's language, why was Episode 18's box estimate off by 1.5?
    Show answer
    The shape of the boundary was wrong. In a box the field spreads in three dimensions and the energy goes as \(\hbar c/R\).
    On a string the field is squeezed to one dimension and the energy is \(\sigma R\) (tension × length) ── a completely different dependence on \(R\).
    ★ A real proton has both (Coulomb-like at short range, string-like at long range ── the Cornell potential \(V=-4\alpha_s/3r+\sigma r\)).
    What this episode establishes is that the long-range side is a string.
  2. Give two pieces of evidence for three colours, and say what each counts.
    Show answer
    ① The R ratio (this episode): the ratio of the hadronic to the muonic cross-section in \(e^+e^-\). It counts how many quark states there are.
    ② The tau lifetime (Episode 19): 5.6 decay channels, three of them hadronic. It counts how many destinations a decay has.
    ★ Both count final states, and both are dimensionless ── Episode 7's rule at work again.
  3. Why is dimensional transmutation "dramatic"?
    Show answer
    Because the input has no dimension and the output does.
    In classical physics a mass scale is always supplied as input (a spring constant, a string length…). In QCD the only input is the dimensionless \(\alpha_s\). Integrate it over \(\ln\mu\) and out comes the scale \(\Lambda\) where the coupling diverges.
    ★ Episode 5's "constants are integrals" has become "the dimension itself is an integral".
    ★ And it is the strongest footnote to Episode 15's "hits are dimensionless, misses are dimensionful" ── dimensionful quantities are built out of dimensionless ones.

Summary　A 14-tonne string, and dimensional transmutation

Episode 18's "box" was really a string (a flux tube). Electromagnetism and Yukawa weaken with distance; only the strong force does not ── hence confinement.

Plot hadron masses as \(M^2\) against \(J\) and they fall on a clean straight line (a Regge trajectory), whose slope gives \(\alpha'=0.915\) GeV⁻² and a string tension of 14.4 tonnes-force. Fourteen tonnes at any distance ── which is why quarks can never be pulled apart. And the string's ladder has the same "boundaries make discrete modes" structure as Episode 6's waveguide and Episode 18's box.

The number of colours is measurable directly as the dimensionless R ratio (\(N_c=3\) fits all three bands) ── agreeing independently with the tau lifetime of Episode 19.

Asymptotic freedom comes from the 11 in \(\beta_0=11-2n_f/3\) (gluon self-interaction). Invert it and ── ★ a dimensionful \(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\) is born from the dimensionless \(\alpha_s\) alone (dimensional transmutation). The corresponding length is 0.94 fm ── the size of a proton.

★ 99 % of the proton's mass is confinement ＝ \(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\),
and \(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}\) was born from a dimensionless number.

And gluons are massless yet reach only 1 fm ── Episode 6's "mass = cutoff" was a linear statement, and in a non-linear theory (Episode 23) confinement happens without mass. Seen as resonances, the \(\rho\)'s width is 19 % of its mass ── the limit of what can be called a wave.

One counterexample would refute it. Twenty-five episodes have supplied the tools, so ★ next time we test it ── eliminate degrees of freedom numerically and see whether a fractional order appears.
