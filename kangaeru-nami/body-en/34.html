04★★ Positivity breaks ── the wave cannot be a free particle

This is the heart of the episode. We read the propagator as a wave response.

An ordinary particle versus a gluon $$\text{Yukawa}:\ D(k)=\frac{1}{k^2+m^2}\qquad\qquad \text{★ Gribov}:\ D(k)=\frac{k^2}{k^4+M^4}$$

The Gribov form vanishes at \(k=0\) and its poles are complex (\(k^2=\pm iM^2\)) ── no real mass

We build the Schwinger function \(C(t)=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty D(k)\cos kt\,dk\) numerically (\(M=0.7\) GeV).

Episodes 19 and 21 said information lives on the imaginary axis. The gluon's poles are not on the real axis but off in the complex plane, so it oscillates as it decays and changes sign.

Exercises

  1. We said light does not scatter light, yet photon–photon scattering exists quantum mechanically. Contradiction?
    Show the answer
    No ── they are at different orders. Classical Maxwell is exactly linear; light does not scatter light at all. Only quantum effects (the Euler–Heisenberg term via an electron loop) produce a tiny nonlinearity at order \(\alpha^4\).
    ★ Episode 16's vacuum birefringence \(\Delta n=3.97\times10^{-24}B^2\) is exactly that size ── \(10^{-33}\) in the Earth's field.
    ★★ Yang–Mills nonlinearity is not a loop correction but the fundamental law itself, and its size is of order \(g^2\). That is the decisive difference.
  2. How should we read the fact that \(D(k)=k^2/(k^4+M^4)\) vanishes at \(k=0\)?
    Show the answer
    "No response at infinite wavelength" ── it does not reach far. An ordinary propagator \(1/(k^2+m^2)\) tends to the finite \(1/m^2\) as \(k\to0\), and that is what gives the Yukawa potential \(e^{-mr}/r\) its range.
    ★ The Gribov form is zero there ── the long-distance response has been removed.
    ★★ And as §04 showed, the price is that the sign flips in the time domain. "Emits nothing at long range" and "cannot be a free particle" are two faces of the same property.
  3. §02 showed chaos. So is confinement caused by chaos once quantized?
    Show the answer
    That does not follow ── this article does not show it. Classical chaos and quantum confinement are different phenomena, and no established argument connects them.
    ★ All we may say is "the same self-interaction produces chaos classically and positivity violation quantum mechanically".
    ★★ This is a boundary worth stating honestly. By this series' own habit (Episodes 29 and 30), "two phenomena from one cause" and "one causes the other" are different claims, and the latter requires content we have not supplied.

Summary　A wave that scatters off itself cannot be a free particle

The difference from Maxwell was one line ── the source contains \(A\) itself; the wave is its own source.

From that line, superposition breaks. Solving the homogeneous SU(2) model for (A) only \(x\), (B) only \(y\), and (C) both, the difference between (C) and (A)+(B) reaches 4.5 by \(t=20\) ── for electromagnetic waves it would be zero always. Energy moves between the two colours while the total is conserved to 9 digits.

★ It is already chaotic classically. The Lyapunov exponent is \(\lambda=0.179\) (against \(2\times10^{-9}\) for the harmonic control) ── one step beyond Episode 23's broken superposition: ★ predictability itself breaks.

The coupling depends on wavelength because the vacuum is a dispersive medium, and \(\beta_0=11-\frac23n_f\) has the opposite sign to electromagnetism ── anti-screening: the wave stiffens the medium.

★★ And the decisive point: the gluon's response function goes negative.
The Gribov \(C(t)\) flips sign at \(t_0=0.3131\) fm (matching the analytic prediction).
The Yukawa form stays positive forever.

★ A positive response function is the condition for a wave to exist as a free particle. Going negative means the gluon cannot exist as a wave on its own ── that is what confinement looks like in the language of waves.

As a bonus, massless yet cut off (the glueball at 1.73 GeV) ── three ways to make a cutoff: a boundary, a background field, or ★ yourself. Episode 25's "two ways" gains a third. Of the linear vocabulary, four items break: superposition, transfer function, predictability and positivity. Causality, energy conservation, gauge invariance and the rule of dimensionless quantities survive.

The model in §01–02 drops the spatial dependence and captures only part of the real field's behaviour. The Gribov form in §04 is one model of the infrared propagator and \(M=0.7\) GeV is a representative value (lattice results depend on the formulation, and the sign-flip time is reported anywhere from 0.3 to 1 fm). Positivity violation itself is seen on the lattice, but "positivity violation ⇒ confinement" is an argument about a necessary condition, not a proof of confinement (the Yang–Mills mass gap problem remains open).
