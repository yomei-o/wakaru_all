Thinking in Waves Episode 4 ／ Part II · Constants as waves

Not a thought experiment. It has already been measured

Differentiating a constant ──
the speed of light became undifferentiable in 1983 "Is the speed of light changing?" has a trap in it. \(c\) is a defined value, so \(dc/dt=0\) is a convention.
Only dimensionless quantities carry meaning ── \(\alpha\), \(\mu=m_p/m_e\), and \(G\).
Their derivatives have actually been measured. And each experiment corresponds to a frequency band.

Last time we framed it as "a physical constant = the \(\omega=0\) component".
So is there anything at \(\omega\ne0\)? That is an experimental question, and the answer is already in.

01The first trap ── \(c\) cannot be measured

The definition since 1983 $$c\ \equiv\ 299\,792\,458\ \text{m/s}\qquad(\text{exactly; a defined value})$$

The metre was defined as the distance light travels in \(1/299792458\) s

Measuring \(c\) is no longer possible in principle. The 2019 SI revision extended this treatment to seven constants.

The heart of §01

These seven differentiate to exactly zero. Because they are definitions.
One cannot even ask whether they are changing.

What is left, then ── dimensionless quantities.
"A universe where \(c\) doubled" is the same universe with a redefined metre.
But "a universe where \(\alpha\) doubled" has different chemistry ── a genuinely different universe.

Relation to the sister series

"Dimensionful quantities are bookkeeping; only dimensionless ones are physics" ── this is the slogan established in Part VI of "Understanding c·t = constant". This episode simply restates it from the wave side; it is not a new claim.
What is this series' own contribution starts here ── translating each experiment into a frequency band.

Conclusion of §03

The lead changes hands at a period of about 32 Gyr (twice the age of the universe). Faster oscillations belong to optical clocks, slower ones to the Oklo natural reactor.

In baseline, cosmological probes win by 8 orders (10 yr vs 1.8 Gyr). In precision, optical clocks win by 9. On balance, a tabletop clock fights the history of the universe to a draw.

★ And as \(f\to0\), every experiment's bound diverges. That is not a failure but a definition ── as we saw last time, the \(\omega=0\) component is the value of the constant.

04But ── the framework itself has a hole

Everything above assumed a stationary sinusoid. Cosmic history is not stationary.

Form of changeContentWhich experiment sees it
A stepone jump at some epoch, constant thereafter★ invisible to clocks. Only CMB and nucleosynthesis
Damped oscillationlarge swings in the early universe, quiet nowsame; scalar-field relaxation models have this shape
Monotonic driftslow change proportional to cosmic agevisible to clocks, but extrapolation is unreliable

The heart of §04 ── the first limit of "see everything as waves"

Fourier is the tool for things that oscillate the same way forever.
Write a one-off event in frequency and it spreads thinly over all frequencies, failing to trip any single band's bound.

⇒ That is why cosmological observations cannot be discarded even when clocks beat them on sensitivity.
This limitation is worth remembering throughout the series ── the language of waves quietly assumes stationarity.

Exercises

  1. How should a claim that "the speed of light has changed" be tested?
    Show answer
    It cannot be ── not as stated. \(c\) is a defined value, so there is nothing to change.
    To make it meaningful one must ask "has \(\alpha=e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c\) changed?" or "has \(m_p/m_e\) changed?". And the moment you translate into a dimensionless quantity, you can no longer tell whether \(c\), \(e\) or \(\hbar\) did the changing.
    This is why varying-speed-of-light (VSL) theories draw the criticism they do.
  2. Why does the bound weaken at low frequency?
    Show answer
    Because a clock measures a rate. An oscillation of amplitude \(A\) and angular frequency \(\omega\) has maximum rate \(A\omega\).
    Given a rate bound \(R\), the amplitude bound is \(Athe smaller \(\omega\), the larger an amplitude is allowed at the same rate.
    Two-epoch comparisons lose sensitivity the same way when \(\omega\Delta t\ll1\): not even half a cycle has elapsed.
  3. Using Episode 1's language, why is a step change hard to catch with Fourier?
    Show answer
    The spectrum of a step goes as \(1/\omega\) and spreads thinly over all frequencies.
    At any single frequency the amplitude is small and trips no bound — yet summed over all bands there is a finite jump.
    This is a direct consequence of the wave language assuming stationarity ── switch to a time-localised basis such as wavelets and you can catch it, but then the notion of "frequency" itself blurs.

Summary　Constants you can differentiate, and constants you cannot

\(c\) has been a defined value since 1983. \(dc/dt=0\) is a convention, not a measurement. The 2019 SI revision gave the same treatment to \(h, e, k_B, N_A\) and others, seven in all. Only dimensionless quantities carry meaning ── \(\alpha\), \(\mu=m_p/m_e\), \(G\).

Their derivatives have been measured ── \(|\dot\alpha/\alpha|<10^{-17}\)/yr, \(|\dot\mu/\mu|<10^{-16}\)/yr, \(|\dot G/G|<10^{-13}\)/yr. Over the whole history of the universe \(\alpha\) has been constant to seven decimal places.

Translated into frequency bands, the lead changes hands at a period of 32 Gyr ── faster oscillations to optical clocks, slower ones to Oklo. And as \(f\to0\) the bound diverges. Naturally: the \(\omega=0\) component is the value of the constant.

But the framework has a hole. Fourier assumes stationarity, so a history like "one jump and then flat" spreads thinly over all frequencies and escapes. That is why cosmological observations cannot be discarded even when they lose on sensitivity.

"Differentiating the constants" is already an experimental programme.
The answer, so far, is zero in every band.

Next time Differentiating with respect to time gave zero. But ── we had the wrong axis.
Next time we differentiate the same \(\alpha\) with respect to energy. The answer is not zero.
\(1/\alpha\) moves from 137.036 to 128.940. And that change is, literally, an integral.
