CONFORMAL TRANSFORMATIONS THAT CLICKEPISODE 3 ／ Rewriting the previous series' motto in the language of the transformation

What exactly did "the speed of light decreases" come out of? Pinning it down in a single equation

What "light slowing down"
actually is The previous series used \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\) from the first page to the last.
Apply a conformal transformation to it and you can see, plainly, where "light slowing down" came from.

What you need: division, exponentials and logarithms, one derivative \(d\tilde s^2=-c_B(t)^2dt^2+dx^2\)

"Cosmology That Clicks" ran on a single auxiliary line from Episode 1 to the finale: read "the universe is expanding" as "the speed of light is slowly decreasing". Bonus episode 4 listed two conditions for that rereading to hold — \(c_B\cdot a=\text{const}\) and invariance of \(\alpha\). The answer was right, but why those two was never made clear. With the conformal transformation prepared in Episodes 1 and 2, both come out of one and the same operation. And the identity of "light slowing down" becomes sayable in a single line.

01A conformal transformation swaps the ruler point by point

As we saw in Episode 1, the conformal (Weyl) transformation fits on one line. At every point of spacetime, multiply the ruler of length by \(\Omega\) — that is all.

The only operation performed in this episode
$$\tilde g_{\mu\nu} = \Omega^2\, g_{\mu\nu}\qquad(\Omega \text{ is a function of place and time})$$

This is not a coordinate transformation (relabelling the same map). A coordinate change leaves the shape of spacetime alone; a conformal transformation stretches length itself. But it stretches equally in all directions at each point, so angles are unchanged — hence "conformal", preserving form.

And as we stressed in Episode 1, the operation can equally be read not as "we changed spacetime" but as "we swapped the ruler". A change of units, carried out point by point. Moving back and forth between these two readings is the theme of the whole series.

02Flattening \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\) in two moves

Set the stage. The metric of a flat expanding (FLRW) universe, written with a lattice that does not stretch (comoving coordinates \(x\)) and a scale factor \(a(t)\), is

$$ds^2 = -c_0^2\,dt^2 + a(t)^2\,dx^2$$

and the previous series' \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\), once unmasked, was \(a(t)=t/t_0\) (linear expansion). We now carry it, by conformal transformation, to a completely flat spacetime. Two moves suffice.

Move one: swap the marks on the time axis (conformal time)

A coordinate change only, to begin with. Use a new time \(\eta\) whose ticks are the old ones divided by \(a\).

Constructing conformal time η

Definition

$$d\eta = \frac{dt}{a(t)} = \frac{t_0}{t}\,dt$$

Integrating

$$\eta = t_0\ln\!\frac{t}{t_0}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad t = t_0e^{\eta/t_0},\quad a(\eta)=e^{\eta/t_0}$$

Something interesting has happened. Expansion that was "linear" in cosmic time, \(a\propto t\), becomes exponential in conformal time, \(a\propto e^{\eta/t_0}\). And the metric takes a form with a single overall factor pulled out front.

$$ds^2 = e^{2\eta/t_0}\Big[-c_0^2\,d\eta^2 + dx^2\Big]$$

Inside the brackets nothing expands any more. It is plain Minkowski (flat) spacetime. The entire expansion has been pushed into the one coefficient \(e^{2\eta/t_0}\) sitting in front.

Move two: erase that coefficient with a conformal transformation

Once a single factor has been pulled out, it is the conformal transformation's turn. Choose \(\Omega = 1/a = e^{-\eta/t_0}\) and it divides out exactly.

The \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\) universe, conformally transformed
$$d\tilde s^2 = \Omega^2 ds^2 = -c_0^2\,d\eta^2 + dx^2$$

Exact Minkowski spacetime. Not a trace of expansion is left. Up to here this is textbook procedure — the most direct way of confirming that FLRW spacetime is conformally flat.

Notice this The moment you use conformal time \(\eta\), light moves at the constant speed \(dx/d\eta = c_0\). On a spacetime diagram, the path of light is a straight line at 45 degrees. — In other words, moving to conformal time makes the speed of light stop changing. The previous series' "light slowing down" was not a statement about conformal time after all. So what was it?

03The heart of it: read the same metric in cosmic time

The \(\tilde g\) we just built is written in the time coordinate \(\eta\). But a metric is the same metric no matter which coordinates you write it in. Let us go back to cosmic time \(t\). All it takes is substituting \(d\eta = (t_0/t)\,dt\).

The same \(\tilde g\), written in t

Substitute

$$-c_0^2\,d\eta^2 = -c_0^2\Big(\frac{t_0}{t}\Big)^2 dt^2 = -\Big(\frac{c_0t_0}{t}\Big)^2 dt^2$$

so that

$$d\tilde s^2 = -\Big(\frac{c_0t_0}{t}\Big)^2 dt^2 + dx^2$$

That coefficient should look familiar. It is the one and only rule from Episode 1 of the previous series.

The rule from Episode 1 of the previous series
$$c(t) = \frac{c_0t_0}{t}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad c\cdot t = c_0t_0 = \text{const}$$

Put them side by side and the answer is already there.

The conclusion of this episode
$$d\tilde s^2 = -c_B(t)^2\,dt^2 + dx^2,\qquad c_B(t)=\frac{c_0t_0}{t}$$

"The speed of light decreases" was the conformally transformed metric, read in cosmic-time coordinates.

One and the same \(\tilde g\): read it in \(\eta\) and the speed of light is constant; read it in \(t\) and the speed of light falls off as \(c_0t_0/t\). The conformal transformation does the job of erasing the expansion, and the choice of time coordinate decides where the price is paid. The picture the previous series called "easy to understand" and kept using was the product of this two-stage move.

As you turn the knob to the right, the marks near the bottom (the early universe) are stretched out more and more, and with them the path of light straightens. Pushing the expansion into the time direction means literally that stretching. And at the far right, once the stretching is complete (conformal time), light travels at the same speed from beginning to end.

What is inside the knob (skippable) The intermediate settings are legitimate time coordinates too. Define \(T_\lambda\) by \(dT_\lambda=\big[(1-\lambda)+\lambda\,t_0/t\big]dt\); then \(\lambda=0\) is cosmic time and \(\lambda=1\) is conformal time. The coordinate speed of light is \(\;c_\lambda = c_0t_0/\big[(1-\lambda)t+\lambda t_0\big]\;\), whose two ends are exactly \(c_0t_0/t\) and \(c_0\). The numbers in the figure come from that formula.

04The matter side: masses grow

So far this has been about the metric — the container. A conformal transformation acts on matter too. As we saw in Episode 2, under \(\tilde g=\Omega^2g\) mass transforms as

$$\tilde m = \Omega^{-1}m = a\,m$$

(this is the only way the mass term \(\int\sqrt{-g}\,m\bar\psi\psi\) can keep its form). Since \(a=t/t_0\) for \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\) —

What is going on in the transformed world
$$\tilde m(t) = m\,\frac{t}{t_0}\qquad(\text{mass grows in proportion to the age of the universe})$$

If mass grows then the Bohr radius \(\hbar/(m c\alpha)\) shrinks — the universe is not expanding; the atoms are shrinking instead. That is Wetterich's picture from Episode 2. And for \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\) the shrinking takes the simplest possible form: proportional to \(1/t\).

Apply the same transformation to temperature and something more startling happens. Temperature is an energy, so \(\tilde T = aT\). But in an expanding universe \(T\propto 1/a\), hence

$$\tilde T = a\cdot\frac{{\rm const}}{a} = \text{constant}$$

The transformed universe does not cool. The cosmic microwave background is 2.725 K in every era. So where did the "cooling of the universe", used throughout Episodes 7, 9 and 10 of the previous series, go? It moved onto the growth of mass. Rather than the temperature dropping until binding energies overtake it, the binding energies grow until they overtake the temperature. The same crossing, seen from the other side.

Check — recombination of hydrogen (z = 1100)

If this is "the same physics", the dimensionless ratios must agree. Recombination is governed by "Rydberg ÷ thermal energy", so let us compute it in both pictures and compare.

The expanding picture (standard)

$$\frac{13.6\ \mathrm{eV}}{k_B\!\cdot\!(2.725\times1101\,\mathrm{K})} = \frac{13.6}{0.2586\ \mathrm{eV}} = 52.6$$

The conformally transformed picture (constant temperature, growing mass)

$$\frac{13.6/1101\ \mathrm{eV}}{k_B\!\cdot\!2.725\,\mathrm{K}} = \frac{0.01235}{2.348\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{eV}} = 52.6$$

Numerator and denominator are both off by a factor 1101, and only the ratio survives. That is what "observation does not change" means.

05Collecting bonus episode 4: "α invariant" was a theorem, not a condition

Bonus episode 4 of the previous series listed two conditions for the equivalence to hold. In the language of the conformal transformation, they are not two separate demands.

Condition 1 (\(c_B\cdot a=\text{const}\)) is nothing but the choice \(\Omega=1/a\) itself. It merely writes out what the transformation is.

Condition 2 (\(\alpha\) invariant) is the interesting one. The ingredients of \(\alpha=e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c\) — \(e\), \(\hbar\) and \(c\) — none of them move under a conformal transformation. The electromagnetic action \(\int\sqrt{-g}\,F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\) is conformally invariant in four dimensions and the \(\Omega\)s cancel completely. Therefore

What condition 2 really is
$$e,\ \hbar,\ c\ \text{have weight }0\ \Longrightarrow\ \alpha\ \text{is automatically invariant}$$

So invariance of \(\alpha\) is not a condition you must protect; it is a theorem that holds by itself once the conformal transformation is done properly. Bonus episode 4 was right to list two conditions — but the second was a consequence of the first.

Then what did VSL get wrong? The varying-speed-of-light theories of bonus episode 3 moved \(c\) alone while holding \(e\) and \(\hbar\) fixed. In the language of conformal transformations: they assigned a weight to \(c\) and none to \(e\) and \(\hbar\). That is not a conformal transformation. So it failed to be a mere rewriting and collided with observation (atomic clocks). The diagnosis "what must be protected is \(\alpha\)" can be restated as "make that operation a conformal transformation".

Being straight with you

A conformal transformation is a rewriting. Rewriting alone produces no new prediction. So this episode does not claim that \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\) is correct — it says only that if \(a\propto t\), then this is what it looks like. Whether the expansion really goes as \(a\propto t\) is a separate matter, settled by observation.

And so far the answer to that separate matter is unkind. Run \(a\propto t\) back into the early universe at face value and nucleosynthesis breaks: the cooling is too slow (Lewis et al. 2016 find a helium mass fraction of order \(10^{-3}\) against the observed 0.25). The bonus episode takes that head on, but note: even with the picture split three ways by the conformal transformation, the verdict is the same in all three — because dimensionless ratios take the same value in every one of them.

Exercises (solvable with this episode's equations alone)
  1. For a galaxy at \(z=1\) (\(t_e=t_0/2\)), what do the three pictures in the table say happened? Give numbers.
    Show answer
    ① Space stretched by 2 and the wavelength doubled. ② The speed of light then was twice today's (\(c_0t_0/t_e=2c_0\)), and that drop is the reddening. ③ The electron mass then was half today's, so the atom emitted at half the frequency. All three give \(1+z=2\).
  2. Under the conformal transformation \(\Omega=1/a\), by what factor does the Bohr radius \(a_B=\hbar/(m c\alpha)\) change? Is it larger or smaller when the universe is young?
    Show answer
    \(\hbar, c, \alpha\) are invariant and \(\tilde m=am\), so \(\tilde a_B=a_B/a\). The smaller \(a\) is (the younger), the larger it is — atoms were bigger in the past and shrink with time. That is the content of "expansion = atoms shrinking".
  3. Explain in one line why the speed of light is constant when read in conformal time.
    Show answer
    Because \(d\eta=dt/a\) was defined that way, so the null condition \(c_0dt=a\,dx\) becomes \(dx/d\eta=c_0\). The erosion by \(a\) has been absorbed in advance into the marks on the time axis.
  4. (Harder) The transformed universe does not cool. How, then, does the Episode 7 story "Big Bang nucleosynthesis happens because the temperature falls" get restated in this picture?
    Show answer
    The temperature stays fixed at 2.725 K while the nucleon mass difference and binding energies grow \(\propto t\) until they overtake the thermal energy \(k_BT\). Not "the temperature drops below a threshold" but "the threshold overtakes the temperature". The moment at which they cross, and the dimensionless ratio at that moment, are exactly the same as in the standard picture.

SUMMARYIt was a two-stage move

Move the \(c\cdot t=\text{const}\) universe to conformal time \(\eta=t_0\ln(t/t_0)\), then apply the conformal transformation \(\Omega=1/a\), and you get exact Minkowski spacetime. Expansion vanishes without trace. Read that same metric back in cosmic time \(t\) and you have \(d\tilde s^2=-c_B(t)^2dt^2+dx^2\) with \(c_B=c_0t_0/t\) — that is what "the speed of light decreases" really is. The conformal transformation erases the expansion; the choice of time coordinate decides where the price is charged.

On the matter side, masses grow \(\propto t\) and the temperature becomes constant: cooling turns into the growth of mass. And because \(e\), \(\hbar\) and \(c\) all sit still under a conformal transformation, \(\alpha\) is invariant without any condition being imposed — bonus episode 4's "condition 2" was in fact a consequence of "condition 1". What VSL stumbled over was failing to make its operation a conformal transformation at all.

That FLRW spacetime is conformally flat, and that in conformal time \(\eta=\int dt/a\) the world lines of light are straight, are standard facts. Under a conformal (Weyl) transformation \(\tilde g=\Omega^2g\), length has weight \(+1\); mass, energy and temperature have weight \(-1\); \(e\), \(\hbar\), \(c\) and the four-dimensional Maxwell action have weight \(0\), so \(\alpha\) is invariant. The formulation of a conformal transformation as a spacetime-dependent change of units is due to Dicke (1962); the cosmology that describes expansion via growing masses is due to Wetterich (2013). On the lack of operational meaning in the time variation of dimensional constants, see Ellis & Uzan (2005). The locally measured speed of light is always \(c_0\) in every description. The "same physics" of this article is an equivalence between ways of speaking (choices of coordinates and units) and does not assert the validity of the model \(a\propto t\) (\(R_h=ct\), linear expansion) itself — for its conflict with nucleosynthesis at face value see Lewis, Barnes & Kaushik (2016, MNRAS 460, 291).
